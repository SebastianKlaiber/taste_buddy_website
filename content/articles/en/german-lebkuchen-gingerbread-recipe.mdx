If your idea of gingerbread is crisp, snappy cut-out gingerbread men, your first bite of **Nuremberg Elisenlebkuchen** will be a revelation. Under German baking standards, *Elisenlebkuchen* is the highest grade of German gingerbread: packed with at least **25% nuts** (hazelnuts, almonds, or walnuts), aromatic candied citrus peel, honey, marzipan, and warming spices—with **zero wheat flour** (or barely a spoonful) in the batter. Baked on thin edible wafer rounds (*Backoblaten*) and dipped in bittersweet chocolate or sugar glaze, they come out cakey, fudgy, deeply spiced, and stay moist for weeks.

This authentic German Lebkuchen recipe includes a homemade **Lebkuchengewürz** (gingerbread spice blend) using low-coumarin Ceylon cinnamon, plus a smart workaround if you can't find wafer rounds at your local store.

## What makes Elisenlebkuchen different from American gingerbread?

- **Nuts replace flour:** Instead of a flour-and-molasses dough you roll and cut, Elisenlebkuchen batter is thick and spoonable—built on ground roasted hazelnuts and almonds whipped with eggs, brown sugar and honey.
- **Complex warm spices:** Traditional *Lebkuchengewürz* layers Ceylon cinnamon, cloves, allspice, coriander, cardamom, nutmeg, ginger and star anise—floral and warming rather than just sweet.
- **Naturally gluten-free option:** This recipe contains **zero flour in the batter**. Simply bake them directly on parchment paper (skipping wheat-based *Oblaten* wafers) for 100% gluten-free Christmas gingerbread.

## Complete Recipe: Soft Nuremberg-Style Elisenlebkuchen

- **Prep time:** 35 minutes (plus 1 hour batter rest)
- **Bake time:** 18 to 20 minutes per tray
- **Total time:** about 2 hours 15 minutes
- **Yield:** about 18 to 20 large Lebkuchen (3-inch / 7 cm rounds)

### Ingredients

**For the homemade Lebkuchen spice blend (or use 1.5 tbsp store-bought Lebkuchengewürz):**
- 2 tsp ground **Ceylon cinnamon** (recommended by the German BfR for low coumarin levels)
- 1/2 tsp ground cloves
- 1/2 tsp ground allspice
- 1/2 tsp ground coriander
- 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
- 1/4 tsp ground ginger
- 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
- Pinch of ground star anise (optional)

**For the Elisenlebkuchen batter:**
- 4 large eggs, at room temperature
- 3/4 cup (150 g) packed light or dark brown sugar
- 2 tbsp (42 g) mild honey
- 4.5 oz (125 g) almond marzipan paste, finely crumbled or grated
- 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
- 1 tsp finely grated untreated orange zest
- 1 tsp finely grated untreated lemon zest
- 2 cups (200 g) finely ground hazelnuts (lightly toasted for deeper flavor)
- 2 cups (200 g) finely ground almonds
- 1/2 cup (90 g) finely minced candied orange peel (*Orangeat*)
- 1/2 cup (90 g) finely minced candied lemon peel (*Zitronat*) — *tip: pulse candied peel with 2 tbsp of the ground nuts in a food processor so it turns super fine with no chewy chunks!*
- 18 to 20 edible baking wafers (*Backoblaten*), 2.75-inch (70 mm) diameter — *or skip and bake directly on parchment paper*

**For the glaze (choose one or split half and half):**
- **Dark chocolate glaze:** 7 oz (200 g) bittersweet chocolate (60–70% cocoa), finely chopped, melted gently with 1 tsp coconut oil or neutral oil
- **Classic sugar glaze:** 1.25 cups (150 g) sifted powdered sugar whisked with 2 to 3 tbsp warm water or fresh orange juice (or dark rum) until glossy
- Optional garnish: 3 blanched whole almonds arranged like a star on top of each glazed Lebkuchen

### Instructions

1. **Mince the candied peel fine:** Pulse the candied orange and lemon peel together with 2 tbsp of the ground almonds in a food processor (or chop very finely with a sharp chef's knife) until almost paste-like. Fine pieces melt seamlessly into the crumb so even candied-peel skeptics love them.
2. **Whip eggs, sugar, honey and marzipan:** In a large stand-mixer bowl (or using a hand mixer), beat the 4 eggs, brown sugar, honey, crumbled marzipan and salt on medium-high speed for **6 to 8 minutes** until thick, pale, foamy and tripled in volume.
3. **Fold in nuts, spices and peel:** In a separate bowl, whisk together the ground hazelnuts, remaining ground almonds, Lebkuchen spice blend, fresh orange and lemon zests, and minced candied peel. Gently fold the nut mixture into the whipped egg foam with a spatula until no dry pockets remain.
4. **Rest the batter:** Cover the bowl and let the batter rest at room temperature for **45 to 60 minutes**. This hydrates the nuts and thickens the mixture so it domes neatly instead of spreading flat.
5. **Shape onto wafers (or parchment):** Preheat the oven to **325°F (165°C)** and line two baking sheets with parchment paper. Arrange the 70 mm *Backoblaten* wafers spaced 1.5 inches (4 cm) apart on the parchment (if baking without wafers, scoop directly onto parchment). Spoon roughly **2 generous tablespoons (about 45 to 50 g)** of batter onto the center of each wafer. Using a dampened small offset spatula or the back of a wet spoon, spread the batter into a neat dome, leaving a **1/8-inch (3 mm)** rim around the wafer edge.
6. **Bake gently:** Bake one tray at a time on the center rack for **18 to 20 minutes**. They are done when puffed, matte on top, lightly golden around the edges, and spring back softly when pressed gently—do **not** overbake, or they lose their fudgy texture!
7. **Cool completely:** Slide the parchment off the hot pan onto a wire rack and let the Lebkuchen cool completely (at least 1 hour) before glazing.
8. **Glaze and set:** Dip the dome of each cooled Lebkuchen into the melted bittersweet chocolate or brush smoothly with the powdered-sugar glaze. Press 3 blanched almonds onto the center if using. Let set uncovered at room temperature for **3 to 4 hours** until the glaze is completely dry to the touch before packing into tins.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Elisenlebkuchen are famous for aging gracefully—they taste noticeably richer and softer **3 to 5 days after baking**. Bake them **2 to 3 weeks before Christmas** right alongside [traditional German Stollen](/en/blog/articles/traditional-german-stollen-recipe/) and [German Zimtsterne](/en/blog/articles/german-zimtsterne-cinnamon-stars/).
- **Storage:** Store completely set Lebkuchen in an airtight metal tin layered with parchment paper in a cool room for **4 to 6 weeks**. Never store soft spiced Lebkuchen in the same tin as crisp [butter cookies](/en/blog/articles/classic-christmas-cookie-recipes/) or [Spritz butter cookies](/en/blog/articles/spritz-butter-cookies-recipe/), or the butter cookies will turn soft and absorb clove spice! Full rules are in our [Christmas cookie storage guide](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/).
- **Common mistake — overbaking:** Lebkuchen look pale and feel soft when you pull them at 19 minutes—that's right! Baking until dark and firm guarantees dry pucks.
- **Common mistake — skipping the egg-whipping time:** Since this batter has zero baking powder, the 6 to 8 minutes of whipping eggs and sugar provides all the lift that keeps the crumb light instead of leaden.
- **Common mistake — coarse candied peel:** Large supermarket cubes of neon citrus peel create bitter chewy pockets. Processing them fine with ground nuts solves this completely.

## FAQ

### Where do I find Backoblaten (baking wafers), and can I bake Lebkuchen without them?
You can find German *Backoblaten* (70 mm) at European specialty grocers or online. If you don't have them, simply scoop and dome the batter directly onto parchment paper—they bake cleanly, release easily once cooled, and become naturally gluten-free!

### Are Elisenlebkuchen naturally gluten-free?
The batter itself contains zero wheat flour—only ground nuts, eggs, sugar, honey, marzipan, candied peel and spices. Just bake them directly on parchment paper instead of standard wheat-based *Oblaten* (or buy certified gluten-free wafers) to keep the finished cookies completely gluten-free.

### What can I substitute if I can't find candied orange and lemon peel?
Use finely chopped dried apricots, dried figs, or dried cranberries pulsed with 2 tbsp ground almonds plus 1 extra tablespoon of fresh grated orange and lemon zest.

### How do I soften Lebkuchen if they dry out slightly?
Place a single wedge of fresh apple on a small square of foil inside the airtight tin for 12 to 24 hours, then remove the apple completely. The Lebkuchen will gently absorb moisture and turn fudgy again.
