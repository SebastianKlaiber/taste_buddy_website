A vegetarian Christmas main dish shouldn't feel like an extra side dish plated in the middle of the table. The best meatless holiday centerpieces have three things in common: deep, savory umami (from browned mushrooms, roasted nuts, miso or red wine), satisfying texture that holds a clean slice, and enough presence that everyone at the table—meat eaters included—wants a piece.

Below are five proven vegetarian and vegan Christmas centerpiece ideas, followed by a complete, bakery-crisp recipe for **Wild Mushroom, Lentil and Chestnut Wellington** (easily 100% vegan with plant-based puff pastry) and a rich Red Wine Onion Gravy.

## 5 vegetarian and vegan Christmas main dish ideas

1. **Wild Mushroom, Lentil & Chestnut Wellington (recipe below):** Flaky puff pastry wrapped around deeply browned mushrooms, earthy green lentils, sweet roasted chestnuts and herbs. Carves cleanly at the table like a roast.
2. **Whole Roasted Stuffed Acorn or Butternut Squash:** Halved squash roasted until caramelized and stuffed with wild rice, toasted pecans, dried cranberries, sage and crispy chickpeas. Naturally gluten-free and vegan.
3. **Caramelized Shallot, Gruyère & Thyme Galette (vegetarian):** Free-form flaky crust layered with jammy balsamic shallots, sautéed cremini mushrooms and nutty Alpine cheese. Great for 4 to 6 guests.
4. **Red-Wine Braised Lentil & Root Vegetable Shepherd's Pie:** Rich mushroom-lentil ragout topped with garlic olive-oil mashed potatoes and baked until golden ridges form. Easily assembled a day ahead.
5. **Herb-Crusted Roasted Cauliflower with Walnut-Cranberry Gremolata:** Whole cauliflower rubbed with smoked paprika, garlic and olive oil, roasted until tender-edged, and served over creamy white bean purée.

Whichever centerpiece you pick, keep the surrounding [classic Christmas side dishes](/en/blog/articles/classic-christmas-side-dishes/) vegetarian by using butter or olive oil and rich vegetable broth instead of meat drippings. That way the entire table shares one cohesive feast.

## Complete Recipe: Wild Mushroom, Lentil & Chestnut Wellington (Vegan-Friendly)

The secret to a crisp-bottomed Wellington that never turns soggy is cooking every drop of moisture out of the mushrooms before wrapping, cooling the filling completely, and baking on a preheated baking sheet. Most commercial puff pastry brands (check the label) use vegetable oil rather than butter, making this recipe effortlessly vegan.

- **Prep time:** 45 minutes (plus 45 minutes cooling/chilling)
- **Cook time:** 40 to 45 minutes
- **Total time:** 2 hours 15 minutes
- **Servings:** 6 to 8

### Ingredients

**For the mushroom, lentil & chestnut filling:**
- 3 tbsp (45 ml) extra-virgin olive oil (or unsalted butter for vegetarian)
- 1.5 lb (680 g) mixed mushrooms (cremini, shiitake and oyster or portobello), finely chopped
- 1 large yellow onion (about 7 oz / 200 g), finely diced
- 3 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary, needles finely chopped (about 1 tbsp)
- 2 tbsp fresh thyme leaves, chopped
- 1 cup (180 g) cooked French green or brown lentils (drained well and patted dry; from about 1/2 cup / 95 g dry)
- 1 cup (150 g) cooked, peeled vacuum-packed chestnuts, finely chopped
- 2 tbsp (30 g) tomato paste
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) soy sauce or tamari
- 1 tbsp (15 g) white or red miso paste (adds deep roast-like savoriness)
- 1/3 cup (80 ml) dry red wine (or vegetable broth)
- 1/2 cup (55 g) panko breadcrumbs (or finely ground oats/walnuts)
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

**For assembly:**
- 1 sheet frozen puff pastry (about 14 to 17 oz / 400 to 480 g; check label for vegan if needed), thawed in the fridge until pliable but still cold
- 2 tbsp (30 g) Dijon mustard (optional, brushed lightly under the filling)
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) unsweetened plant milk mixed with 1 tsp maple syrup for vegan glaze (or 1 beaten egg for vegetarian)
- 1/2 tsp flaky sea salt for sprinkling

**For the quick red wine onion gravy:**
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) olive oil or butter
- 2 medium yellow onions, thinly sliced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp (8 g) all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) dry red wine
- 2 cups (480 ml) rich vegetable broth
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) soy sauce or tamari
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 tsp balsamic vinegar

### Instructions

1. **Brown the mushrooms deeply:** Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in a wide 12-inch (30 cm) skillet over medium-high heat. Add the finely chopped mushrooms with a pinch of salt and cook undisturbed for 3 minutes, then stir frequently for **10 to 12 minutes** until all their liquid has evaporated and the mushrooms are deeply browned. Transfer to a bowl.
2. **Build the savory base:** Add the remaining 1 tbsp oil to the same skillet over medium heat. Sauté the diced onion for 6 to 7 minutes until soft and lightly golden. Add garlic, rosemary, thyme and tomato paste; cook 2 minutes until fragrant and darkened.
3. **Deglaze and combine:** Pour in the red wine, scraping up any browned bits, and simmer 2 minutes until nearly dry. Return the mushrooms to the pan and stir in the lentils, chopped chestnuts, soy sauce, miso paste and black pepper. Cook 3 minutes, gently mashing about one-third of the lentils with the back of a spoon so the mixture binds together.
4. **Dry out and cool completely:** Remove from the heat and stir in the panko breadcrumbs—the filling should feel like a cohesive, moldable paste, not wet or crumbly. Taste and adjust salt. Spread it on a plate and chill in the refrigerator for **at least 30 minutes** until completely cool (warm filling melts puff pastry butter and prevents puffing).
5. **Shape and wrap:** Preheat the oven to **400°F (200°C)** with a bare metal baking sheet already inside to heat up (hot metal guarantees a crisp bottom crust). On a sheet of parchment paper, roll the cold puff pastry into a rectangle roughly **11 × 15 inches (28 × 38 cm)**. Lightly brush the center strip with Dijon mustard if using.
6. **Form the log:** Mound the chilled filling down the center third of the pastry, shaping it into a compact, even log about **12 inches (30 cm) long and 3.5 inches (9 cm) wide**, leaving a 1.5-inch (4 cm) border at both short ends.
7. **Seal tightly:** Brush the exposed pastry edges with the plant-milk glaze (or beaten egg). Fold the two short ends neatly over the filling, then fold one long side up and over, and wrap the other long side smoothly over top to overlap by 1 inch (2.5 cm). Gently roll the entire parcel over onto the parchment so the seam sits underneath.
8. **Chill, score and bake:** Chill the assembled Wellington for 15 minutes if the pastry feels soft. Brush the top and sides with glaze, score a shallow diagonal lattice pattern across the top with a paring knife (do not cut all the way through), cut 3 small steam vents along the center, and sprinkle with flaky sea salt. Slide the parchment directly onto the hot baking sheet in the oven and bake for **38 to 45 minutes**, until deeply golden brown and puffed.
9. **Make the onion gravy while it bakes:** Heat 2 tbsp oil or butter in a heavy saucepan over medium heat. Add sliced onions with a pinch of salt and cook **15 to 18 minutes**, stirring often, until soft and deeply caramelized. Stir in garlic and flour for 1 minute, deglaze with red wine, then whisk in vegetable broth, soy sauce and thyme. Simmer 10 minutes until glossy; remove thyme, stir in balsamic vinegar, and season to taste.
10. **Rest and slice:** Let the Wellington rest on the baking sheet for **10 minutes** before slicing with a sharp serrated bread knife into 1.5-inch (4 cm) thick slices. Serve immediately with warm red wine onion gravy.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** You can cook the mushroom-lentil-chestnut filling up to **2 days ahead** and keep it tightly covered in the fridge—cold filling is actually easier to shape. Assemble the Wellington up to 12 hours ahead, cover loosely in the fridge, and glaze right before baking (add 5 minutes to the bake time). See our [make-ahead Christmas dinner guide](/en/blog/articles/make-ahead-christmas-dinner/) for full menu prep timelines.
- **Storage:** Cool leftovers within 2 hours and refrigerate in a container for **3 to 4 days** (USDA guideline). Reheat slices on a baking sheet in a 375°F (190°C) oven for 12 to 15 minutes so the pastry crisps back up—avoid the microwave, which softens puff pastry. More remix ideas are in [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — wet mushroom filling:** Undercooked mushrooms leak water in the oven and turn the bottom crust gummy. Cook mushrooms until the pan is completely dry and sizzling before adding wine or lentils.
- **Common mistake — slicing immediately out of the oven:** Resting for 10 minutes lets the steam settle and the lentil-chestnut filling firm up so slices stay neat on the plate.

## FAQ

### Can I make this Mushroom Wellington completely vegan?
Yes. Use olive oil instead of butter, check that your store-bought puff pastry uses plant-based fats (most standard supermarket brands like Pepperidge Farm do), and brush the outside with unsweetened plant milk mixed with a little maple syrup for rich golden browning.

### Can I freeze a vegetarian Mushroom Wellington before baking?
Yes. Assemble the unbaked Wellington (skip the glaze), wrap tightly in parchment and foil, and freeze for up to 1 month. Bake straight from frozen at 400°F (200°C) for 55 to 65 minutes, tenting loosely with foil if the top browns too quickly.

### What if someone at the table has a nut allergy?
Replace the 1 cup (150 g) of chopped chestnuts with roasted finely diced butternut squash or extra cooked green lentils and 2 tbsp toasted sunflower seeds. The texture stays hearty and sliceable.

### What side dishes go best with a vegetarian Christmas main?
Sweet-sour braised red cabbage, garlic mashed potatoes or potato dumplings, maple-roasted carrots and parsnips, and crispy Brussels sprouts balance the rich puff pastry and earthy mushrooms.
