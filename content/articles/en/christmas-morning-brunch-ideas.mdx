Christmas morning shouldn't start with someone flipping 30 pancakes over a splattering griddle while everyone else opens stockings. The best **Christmas morning brunch ideas** follow one simple rule: **assemble on December 24, refrigerate overnight, and slide straight into the oven while the coffee brews** on December 25.

Below is a balanced, low-effort Christmas brunch spread (sweet, savory, fruit and drinks) that doesn't spoil anyone's appetite for dinner, followed by two complete make-ahead centerpiece recipes: an **Overnight Spiced Apple & Brioche French Toast Bake** and a savory **Smoked Gouda, Spinach & Mushroom Breakfast Strata**.

## How to build a calm Christmas morning brunch spread

Keep Christmas brunch to **four simple pieces** so you aren't washing a mountain of dishes before starting [Christmas dinner](/en/blog/articles/christmas-dinner-menu-ideas/):

1. **One warm overnight bake (sweet or savory):** Put it in the oven as soon as you wake up—zero active cooking while people unwrap gifts.
2. **One cold savory board:** Sliced smoked salmon (or avocado), bagels or warm croissants, whipped cream cheese, crisp cucumber and capers.
3. **One bright winter fruit platter:** Blood orange and clementine segments, pomegranate seeds, sliced pears and grapes to balance rich holiday dishes.
4. **One festive drink station:** Freshly brewed coffee, hot cocoa, sparkling cranberry-orange mimosas (or non-alcoholic sparkling apple cider), plus thin slices of [traditional German Stollen](/en/blog/articles/traditional-german-stollen-recipe/) or a few [classic Christmas cookies](/en/blog/articles/classic-christmas-cookie-recipes/) for early nibbling.

## Recipe 1: Overnight Spiced Apple & Brioche French Toast Bake

Cube up sturdy brioche or challah the night before, tuck in quick sautéed cinnamon apples, pour over vanilla-maple custard, and wake up to a golden, soufflé-puffed casserole crowned with brown-sugar pecan streusel.

- **Prep time:** 25 minutes (on December 24) + overnight chill (8 to 14 hours)
- **Bake time:** 45 to 50 minutes (unattended on Christmas morning)
- **Total time:** 1 hour 15 minutes (excluding overnight soak)
- **Servings:** 8

### Ingredients

**For the French toast base:**
- 1 large loaf (14 to 16 oz / 400 to 450 g) day-old brioche, challah, or slightly stale [German Stollen](/en/blog/articles/traditional-german-stollen-recipe/), cut into 1-inch (2.5 cm) cubes
- 2 medium firm tart apples (Honeycrisp or Granny Smith), peeled, cored and diced into 1/2-inch (1.2 cm) cubes
- 2 tbsp (28 g) unsalted butter (plus extra for greasing the dish)
- 2 tbsp (25 g) light brown sugar
- 1 tsp ground Ceylon cinnamon, divided
- 6 large eggs
- 2 cups (480 ml) whole milk
- 1 cup (240 ml) heavy cream (or half-and-half)
- 1/3 cup (80 ml) pure maple syrup, plus warm syrup for serving
- 2 tsp pure vanilla extract
- 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
- 1/2 tsp fine sea salt

**For the crisp cinnamon-pecan streusel (mixed night before, scattered before baking):**
- 1/2 cup (60 g) all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup (100 g) packed light brown sugar
- 1/2 cup (55 g) coarsely chopped pecans or walnuts
- 1 tsp ground Ceylon cinnamon
- Pinch of fine sea salt
- 4 tbsp (56 g) cold unsalted butter, cut into tiny cubes

### Instructions

1. **Sauté the apples lightly:** Melt 2 tbsp butter in a skillet over medium heat. Add the diced apples, 2 tbsp brown sugar and 1/2 tsp cinnamon. Cook **5 to 6 minutes** until just tender at the edges (not mushy). Let cool for 10 minutes.
2. **Assemble in the dish:** Butter a 9 × 13-inch (23 × 33 cm) baking dish generously. Scatter half the cubed brioche across the bottom, spoon half the cooled spiced apples over it, then layer the remaining brioche and remaining apples on top.
3. **Whisk and pour the custard:** In a large bowl, whisk together the 6 eggs, whole milk, heavy cream, 1/3 cup maple syrup, vanilla extract, remaining 1/2 tsp cinnamon, nutmeg and salt until completely smooth. Pour the custard evenly over the bread, pressing the top cubes gently with the back of a spatula so every piece absorbs liquid. Cover tightly and refrigerate **8 to 14 hours** overnight.
4. **Make the streusel ahead:** In a small bowl, rub the flour, brown sugar, chopped pecans, cinnamon, salt and cold cubed butter together with your fingertips until coarse crumbs form. Cover and refrigerate in a separate small bag or bowl overnight.
5. **Bake on Christmas morning:** Remove the casserole from the fridge 20 minutes before baking while the oven preheats to **350°F (175°C)**. Uncover the dish, scatter the chilled pecan streusel evenly over the top, and bake uncovered on the center rack for **45 to 50 minutes**, until puffed, deeply golden, and the center reaches at least **160°F (71°C)** on an instant-read thermometer (the USDA safe minimum internal temperature for egg-based casseroles—a knife inserted in the center should show no runny raw egg). If the streusel browns quickly after 35 minutes, tent loosely with foil.
6. **Rest and serve:** Let rest **10 minutes** so the custard sets cleanly, dust lightly with powdered sugar, and serve warm with maple syrup and fresh berries or Greek yogurt.

## Recipe 2: Savory Overnight Spinach, Mushroom & Gruyère Breakfast Strata

If your crew prefers savory over sweet—or you're hosting a big house full of relatives—bake this herb-scented strata alongside the French toast bake (they bake at the exact same **350°F / 175°C** temperature!).

- **Prep time:** 25 minutes (night before) + overnight soak
- **Bake time:** 45 to 50 minutes
- **Servings:** 8

### Ingredients

- 12 oz (340 g) day-old crusty sourdough or country bread, cut into 1-inch (2.5 cm) cubes
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) extra-virgin olive oil
- 10 oz (280 g) cremini mushrooms, thinly sliced
- 1 medium yellow onion (or 2 shallots), finely diced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 5 oz (140 g) fresh baby spinach
- 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (or 1 tsp dried)
- 2 cups (200 g) coarsely grated Gruyère or sharp white cheddar cheese, divided
- 8 large eggs
- 2.25 cups (540 ml) whole milk (or 1.75 cups milk + 1/2 cup heavy cream)
- 1 tbsp (15 g) Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp kosher salt and 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

### Instructions

1. **Cook out moisture from mushrooms and spinach:** Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Sauté the sliced mushrooms and diced onion with a pinch of salt for **7 to 8 minutes** until browned and completely dry. Add garlic, thyme and baby spinach, stirring 2 minutes until wilted and dry. Cool slightly.
2. **Layer and soak overnight:** Grease a 9 × 13-inch (23 × 33 cm) baking dish. Layer half the bread cubes, half the mushroom-spinach mixture, and half the grated Gruyère; repeat with the remaining bread, vegetables and cheese. Whisk together the eggs, milk, Dijon mustard, salt and pepper, pour evenly over the strata, press down gently, cover, and refrigerate **8 to 14 hours**.
3. **Bake until set and golden:** Preheat the oven to **350°F (175°C)**. Uncover and bake for **45 to 50 minutes** until puffed, golden brown on top, and the center reaches **160°F (71°C)** (USDA safe egg-casserole temperature). Rest 10 minutes, garnish with snipped chives, and cut into squares.

## Make-ahead, food safety and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Both bakes must be assembled the evening before—in fact, the overnight soak is what turns bread into a custardy pudding instead of dry cubes floating in scrambled egg.
- **Food safety for egg bakes:** Keep assembled unbaked egg casseroles continuously refrigerated at **40°F (4°C) or below** until baking, cook them to **160°F (71°C)** in the center, and refrigerate leftovers within **2 hours** of serving (USDA guidance). Enjoy leftover squares within **3 to 4 days**—reheat in a 350°F (175°C) oven for 12 minutes so the edges crisp back up. Find more post-holiday ideas in [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — using super-soft fresh sandwich bread:** Fresh floppy white bread turns gummy overnight. Slightly stale (1- to 2-day-old) brioche, challah or sourdough—or fresh cubes lightly toasted at 300°F (150°C) for 10 minutes—holds its airy structure.
- **Common mistake — putting cold glass straight into a blazing hot oven:** If using a glass baking dish, let it sit on the counter for 20 minutes while the oven preheats so thermal shock doesn't crack the glass.

## FAQ

### Can I bake the sweet French Toast Bake and savory Strata at the same time?
Yes! Both bake at **350°F (175°C)** for 45 to 50 minutes. Place them side by side on the center rack (or swap top and bottom racks halfway through if using two racks) and confirm both centers reach 160°F (71°C).

### How can I make the Overnight French Toast Bake dairy-free?
Swap the brioche for dairy-free sourdough or challah, use vegan block butter in the streusel and apples, and substitute full-fat oat milk + canned light coconut milk for the milk and cream.

### What if I forgot to assemble the French Toast Bake the night before?
You can still make it on Christmas morning! Cut the bread slightly smaller (3/4-inch cubes), press them well into the custard, and let the dish soak at room temperature for **30 to 45 minutes** before adding the streusel and baking.

### How early should we eat Christmas brunch if we're having a big Christmas dinner?
Aim to serve brunch around **9:30 to 10:30 AM** and keep portions moderate if your main Christmas dinner is at 4:00 or 5:00 PM—that gives everyone a comfortable 6-hour gap to build an appetite again.
