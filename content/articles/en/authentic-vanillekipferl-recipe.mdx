Walk into any bakery in Vienna, Munich or Nuremberg in December and **Vanillekipferl** are the first cookies to sell out. Crisp on the outside for half a second before dissolving into pure toasted almond, butter and real vanilla bean on your tongue, these little crescent cookies are the heart of Central European holiday baking.

They are also famous for two frustrations: dough that crumbles when you try to roll crescents, and fragile baked crescents that snap in half while being tossed in vanilla sugar. Both problems are completely solved by getting the butter-to-nut ratio right, chilling the dough properly, and coating them at the exact right moment—**while still warm, never piping hot**.

## What makes a Vanillekipferl authentic?

- **No baking powder and no whole eggs.** Traditional Vanillekipferl rely on a rich, eggless (or single-yolk) shortbread dough packed with finely ground almonds (or walnuts/hazelnuts). Skipping egg whites is what creates that sandy, melt-in-your-mouth *mürbe* texture.
- **Finely ground blanched almonds (or half hazelnuts).** Classic Viennese versions use blanched almonds or walnuts; many German family recipes use half almonds and half hazelnuts for a deeper toasted note.
- **Real vanilla bean sugar.** Rolling the warm cookies in a mix of powdered sugar and real vanilla sugar (made with scraped vanilla bean seeds) gives Vanillekipferl their unmistakable snowy cloak and perfume.

## Complete Recipe: Authentic German & Austrian Vanillekipferl

- **Prep time:** 35 minutes (plus 1 hour chilling)
- **Bake time:** 10 to 12 minutes per tray
- **Total time:** about 2 hours
- **Yield:** about 45 to 50 crescents

### Ingredients

**For the crescent dough:**
- 1.75 cups plus 2 tbsp (225 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 cup (100 g) finely ground blanched almonds (almond flour—not coarse almond meal)
- 1/3 cup plus 1 tbsp (75 g) granulated sugar (or superfine caster sugar)
- 1 cup minus 1.5 tbsp (200 g) cold unsalted butter, cut into small cubes
- 1 large egg yolk (optional—makes shaping easier for beginners; classic Austrian versions omit it, and both work cleanly here)
- Seeds scraped from 1/2 vanilla bean (or 1 tsp pure vanilla bean paste)
- 1/4 tsp fine sea salt

**For the snowy vanilla sugar coating:**
- 3/4 cup (90 g) powdered sugar (confectioners' sugar), sifted
- 2 tbsp (25 g) real vanilla sugar (store-bought *Vanillezucker*, or blend 2 tbsp granulated sugar with the seeds of the remaining 1/2 vanilla bean)

### Instructions

1. **Mix the dough quickly:** In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the all-purpose flour, finely ground almonds, granulated sugar, scraped vanilla bean seeds and salt. Scatter the cold cubed butter (and egg yolk, if using) over top. Using cold fingertips or a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment on low speed, work the mixture quickly until it clumps together, then press with your hands into a smooth, uniform dough—don't over-knead once it holds together.
2. **Chill thoroughly:** Divide the dough in half, shape into two flat disks, wrap tightly, and refrigerate for **at least 60 minutes** (up to 48 hours). Cold fat is essential so the crescents hold their curved horns in the oven.
3. **Prepare the coating and preheat:** Preheat the oven to **350°F (175°C)** and line two baking sheets with parchment paper. Sift the powdered sugar and vanilla sugar together into a wide shallow dish or soup plate.
4. **Roll uniform crescents (the crack-free trick):** Work with one half of the chilled dough at a time, keeping the other half in the fridge. If the dough feels rock-hard straight out of the fridge, let it sit 5 minutes and knead it briefly in your palms until just pliable. Pinch off pieces about **1/2 oz (12 to 14 g)** each (roughly the size of a walnut). Roll each piece between your palms into a smooth ball first—this eliminates cracks—then roll on an unfloured board into a **2.5-inch (6 cm) log** that tapers gently at both ends. Curve the log into a gentle crescent (U-shape) and set it on the parchment-lined sheet, leaving 1 inch (2.5 cm) between cookies.
5. **Bake until pale ivory:** Bake one tray at a time on the center rack for **10 to 12 minutes**. Pull the tray when the crescents are pale ivory on top and the very tips are just beginning to turn light golden—never wait for the whole cookie to brown, or they turn dry and bitter.
6. **Rest 3 minutes, then coat while warm:** Directly out of the oven, Vanillekipferl are extremely fragile—do not touch them yet! Let them rest undisturbed on the baking sheet for **3 to 4 minutes** until just firm enough to lift with a thin spatula, while still noticeably warm.
7. **Coat gently:** Rather than tossing handfuls in a bowl (which snaps the tips), either gently lay 4 to 5 warm crescents at a time into the dish of vanilla-powdered sugar and spoon sugar over them, or sift a generous snowstorm of vanilla sugar directly over them on the tray, flip gently once cooled another 2 minutes, and sift again. Transfer to a wire rack to cool completely.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Vanillekipferl dough keeps tightly wrapped in the fridge for **3 days** or in the freezer for **2 months**. Even better: Vanillekipferl taste *better* after sitting in a tin for 3 to 5 days as the vanilla aroma permeates the butter-almond crumb.
- **Storage:** Layer cooled Vanillekipferl gently between sheets of parchment paper in an airtight metal cookie tin in a cool room. They stay tender and fragrant for **3 to 4 weeks**. Don't stack heavy cookies on top of them, and check our [Christmas cookie storage and baking timeline](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/) for storing different cookie types together.
- **Food safety note:** Per FDA guidance, avoid eating raw cookie dough due to unbaked flour (and raw egg yolk if used).
- **Common mistake — rolling crescents too thin at the tips:** Needle-thin tips burn 3 minutes before the center bakes through. Keep the tapered ends slightly blunted, about the thickness of a pencil.
- **Common mistake — waiting until the cookies are cold to sugar them:** Cold butter cookies repel powdered sugar. Coating them 3 to 4 minutes out of the oven lets the residual warmth melt the very first dusting microscopically so the snowy coat adheres cleanly.
- **Common mistake — using coarse almond meal with skins:** Dark flecks look rustic, but coarse meal makes the dough crack while shaping. Fine blanched almond flour gives the classic silky snap.

## FAQ

### Why did my Vanillekipferl spread flat in the oven?
The dough warmed up too much during shaping or the butter was softened before mixing. Always start with cold cubed butter, chill the shaped tray in the fridge for 10 minutes before baking if your hands warmed the dough, and verify your oven temperature with an oven thermometer.

### Can I make Vanillekipferl with walnuts or hazelnuts instead of almonds?
Yes! Finely ground walnuts give a classic Austrian earthiness, while finely ground roasted hazelnuts create a deeper, nuttier flavor. Substitute 1:1 by weight (100 g).

### How do I make Vanillekipferl vegan?
Skip the optional egg yolk (classic Austrian Vanillekipferl are already egg-free!) and replace the 200 g dairy butter with **200 g firm, high-fat vegan block butter** (at least 75–80% fat, not tub margarine). You'll find more ideas in our [gluten-free and vegan Christmas cookies guide](/en/blog/articles/gluten-free-vegan-christmas-cookies/).

### How long do Vanillekipferl keep fresh?
Stored in an airtight metal tin lined with parchment paper in a cool room, Vanillekipferl keep beautifully for **3 to 4 weeks**—and they actually taste peak-tender about 4 to 7 days after baking.
