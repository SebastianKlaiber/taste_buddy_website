**Zimtsterne**—German cinnamon stars—are pure holiday baking magic: zero flour, zero butter, naturally gluten-free and dairy-free, topped with a crisp snow-white meringue glaze and hiding a soft, marzipan-like spiced almond center underneath. They are also the single best way to use up leftover egg whites after baking [classic butter cookies](/en/blog/articles/classic-christmas-cookie-recipes/) or [Vanillekipferl](/en/blog/articles/authentic-vanillekipferl-recipe/).

The catch? Anyone who has tried making Zimtsterne knows the two classic traps: dough that glues itself hopelessly to the star cutter, and meringue tops that turn beige and rock-hard in the oven. Here is the foolproof method—using **low-temperature baking**, **powdered sugar instead of flour for rolling**, and **Ceylon cinnamon**—for chewy, snow-white Zimtsterne every time.

## Why Ceylon cinnamon matters for Zimtsterne

Zimtsterne use a generous amount of cinnamon—more than almost any other cookie. Food-safety authorities like the German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) recommend using **Ceylon cinnamon** ("true cinnamon") rather than common Cassia cinnamon whenever baking cinnamon-heavy holiday treats, because Ceylon cinnamon is naturally very low in **coumarin** (a plant compound that should be limited in large doses, especially for children) and tastes subtly sweeter and more floral.

## Complete Recipe: Soft, Chewy German Zimtsterne

- **Prep time:** 40 minutes (plus 1 hour chilling + optional 2 hours drying before baking)
- **Bake time:** 12 to 15 minutes at low heat
- **Total time:** about 2 hours 15 minutes
- **Yield:** about 35 to 40 stars (2-inch / 5 cm cutter)

### Ingredients

- 3 large egg whites (about 100 g), at room temperature
- Pinch of fine sea salt
- 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
- 2 cups (250 g) powdered sugar (confectioners' sugar), sifted, plus extra for dusting while rolling
- 3.5 cups (350 to 380 g) finely ground almonds (blanched almond flour or skin-on finely ground almonds—start with 350 g and add up to 30 g more if needed)
- 2.5 tsp ground **Ceylon cinnamon**
- 1 tsp finely grated untreated lemon or orange zest (optional, lifts the spice beautifully)
- 1/2 tsp pure vanilla extract or 1 tsp kirsch/amaretto (optional)

### Instructions

1. **Whip glossy, stiff meringue:** Make sure your mixing bowl and beaters are completely clean and grease-free. Beat the 3 egg whites with a pinch of salt and 1 tsp lemon juice on medium-high speed until foamy and soft peaks form. Gradually add the **250 g sifted powdered sugar**, two tablespoons at a time, beating continuously for **4 to 5 minutes** until the meringue is thick, bright snow-white, and holds stiff glossy peaks.
2. **Reserve the glaze first!** Scoop out **1/2 cup (about 80 to 90 g)** of the glossy meringue into a small bowl, cover tightly with plastic wrap pressed directly onto the surface (so it doesn't crust over), and set aside for glazing the tops.
3. **Make the cinnamon-almond dough:** In a separate bowl, whisk together 350 g ground almonds, the Ceylon cinnamon and lemon zest. Fold this almond mixture into the remaining meringue in two additions using a sturdy spatula (and finish by hand if needed) until a thick, slightly tacky marzipan-like dough forms. If the dough feels too wet to shape, fold in the extra 20 to 30 g ground almonds—stop while it still feels slightly soft and moist, because dry dough yields dry stars.
4. **Chill the dough:** Flatten the dough into a disk, wrap tightly, and chill in the refrigerator for **60 minutes** (firm, cold dough cuts cleanly with far less sticking).
5. **Roll out on powdered sugar (never flour):** Dust a piece of parchment paper generously with powdered sugar (or ground almonds) and lay a second piece of parchment on top of the chilled dough. Roll the dough evenly to **1/3 inch (8 mm)** thick—thick stars stay soft and chewy inside!
6. **Cut stars without sticking:** Keep a small glass of cold water and a saucer of powdered sugar beside your board. Dip your star cutter into cold water (shake off drops) or powdered sugar before **every second cut**. Lift each star cleanly onto a parchment-lined baking sheet using a thin palette knife or offset spatula. Press scraps gently together, re-chill 10 minutes if soft, and cut the rest.
7. **Glaze the tops neatly:** Stir 1/2 tsp water or lemon juice into your reserved meringue only if it feels too stiff to spread. Using a small flat paintbrush, a toothpick, or a small piping bag with a tiny tip, spread a neat layer of white meringue onto every star, pulling it carefully out into all five or six points. (Optional traditional step: let the glazed stars sit uncovered at room temperature for 1 to 2 hours so the glaze dries into a crisp shell before baking.)
8. **Bake low and slow:** Preheat the oven to **275°F to 300°F (135°C to 150°C — conventional, no fan)**—lower heat keeps the glaze stark white while gently setting the almond base. Bake one tray at a time on the lower-middle rack for **12 to 15 minutes**. Pull the tray when the meringue is dry to the touch and **still pure white**, and the bottoms just release cleanly from the parchment while the centers still feel slightly soft when nudged.
9. **Cool completely:** Slide the parchment sheet off the hot pan onto a wire rack and let the Zimtsterne cool completely—the centers set to a fudgy, chewy consistency as they cool.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Zimtsterne are ideal for baking **2 to 3 weeks before Christmas**—their almond-cinnamon flavor actually mellows and deepens after a few days in a tin.
- **Storage:** Store completely cooled Zimtsterne in an airtight metal cookie tin layered with parchment paper in a cool room for **3 to 4 weeks**. If they ever feel slightly firmer than you'd like after a few weeks, tuck a fresh apple wedge on a piece of foil inside the tin overnight (remove after 24 hours so it never molds)—the stars absorb just enough moisture to turn chewy again! Read more in our [Christmas cookie storage and baking timeline](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/).
- **Common mistake — baking at 350°F (175°C):** Standard cookie temperatures brown the meringue glaze yellow-beige and bake the chewy center bone-dry. Always bake Zimtsterne at **275°F–300°F (135°C–150°C)**.
- **Common mistake — rolling the dough too thin:** Rolling to 1/8 inch like butter cookies guarantees hard, brittle stars. Keep Zimtsterne thick—a full **1/3 inch (8 mm)**.
- **Common mistake — adding too much almond flour:** The dough *should* feel slightly sticky before chilling. Overloading it with dry almonds creates crumbly stars that crack at the points.

## FAQ

### Are German Zimtsterne naturally gluten-free and dairy-free?
Yes! Traditional Zimtsterne contain only egg whites, powdered sugar, ground almonds and cinnamon—zero wheat flour and zero butter. Just check that your powdered sugar and spices are certified gluten-free if baking for someone with celiac disease.

### Why did my Zimtsterne turn hard as rocks?
Either the oven temperature was too high (above 300°F / 150°C), they baked too long, or the dough was rolled too thin. Keep them 1/3 inch (8 mm) thick, bake low at 275°F–300°F for just 12 to 15 minutes, and pull them while the centers still feel soft.

### Can I make Zimtsterne with hazelnuts instead of almonds?
Yes. Swapping half (or all) of the ground almonds for finely ground roasted hazelnuts makes a rich, nutty variation—just keep the total nut weight at 350 to 380 g.

### Is the egg white meringue glaze safe to eat after low-temperature baking?
Yes—baking at 275°F–300°F (135°C–150°C) for 12 to 15 minutes brings the thin meringue layer well above the USDA safe egg temperature of 160°F (71°C). If you want extra peace of mind, you can also use pasteurized liquid egg whites (choose a brand labeled "whippable").
