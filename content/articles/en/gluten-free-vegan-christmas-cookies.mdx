Making **vegan gluten-free Christmas cookies** doesn't require hunting down five obscure gums or specialty flours that leave a gritty aftertaste. The best plant-based, gluten-free holiday cookies lean on ingredients that already define European Christmas baking—**finely ground almonds, roasted hazelnuts, dark cocoa, maple syrup, citrus zest and warm spices**—so nobody at the cookie platter even realizes they're allergy-friendly.

Below are two completely different, bakery-quality vegan and gluten-free Christmas cookie recipes that use simple grocery-store ingredients: **Almond-Orange Snowball Cookies** (tender, melt-in-your-mouth, 6 ingredients) and **Fudgy Chocolate-Hazelnut Crinkle Cookies** (crisp crackled snowy crust, brownie-soft center).

## Essential tips for baking vegan & gluten-free cookies

- **Choose naturally gluten-free bases:** Almond flour, hazelnut meal and certified gluten-free oat flour build richness and structure effortlessly—no chalky rice-flour blends needed.
- **Prevent cross-contact for celiac guests:** If you're baking for someone with celiac disease, wash mixing bowls, cookie sheets and utensils thoroughly in hot soapy water, line every tray with fresh parchment paper, and use unopened certified gluten-free baking powder, spices and powdered sugar (per Celiac Disease Foundation and FDA cross-contact guidance).
- **Use firm block vegan butter (when butter is called for):** Soft tub margarine holds too much water and makes cookies spread flat. Look for stick/block plant-based butter with 75–80% fat—or rely on nut oils and aquafaba/flax binders like the two foolproof recipes below.

## Recipe 1: Melt-in-Your-Mouth Almond-Orange Snowball Cookies (6 Ingredients)

Think of these as a bright, citrusy cousin to [authentic Vanillekipferl](/en/blog/articles/authentic-vanillekipferl-recipe/) and Italian *ricciarelli*—zero butter, zero oil, zero eggs, zero flour, yet wonderfully tender and chewy inside with a crisp powdered-sugar snowfall outside.

- **Prep time:** 15 minutes (plus 30 minutes chilling)
- **Bake time:** 12 to 14 minutes
- **Total time:** about 1 hour
- **Yield:** about 22 cookies

### Ingredients

- 2.25 cups (225 g) finely ground blanched almond flour (super-fine)
- 1/2 cup (60 g) certified gluten-free powdered sugar (for the dough), plus 1/2 cup (60 g) extra for rolling
- 1 tbsp (12 g) finely grated untreated orange zest (from 1 large navel orange)
- 1/4 tsp fine sea salt
- 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon or cardamom (optional)
- 1/4 cup (60 ml) freshly squeezed orange juice
- 2 tbsp (40 g) pure maple syrup (or agave syrup)
- 1/2 tsp pure almond extract (optional, intensifies the marzipan aroma)

### Instructions

1. **Mix dry ingredients:** In a medium bowl, whisk together the blanched almond flour, 60 g powdered sugar, orange zest, salt and cinnamon/cardamom until completely lump-free.
2. **Stir into a soft marzipan dough:** Add the fresh orange juice, maple syrup and almond extract (if using). Stir vigorously with a sturdy spoon or spatula until a thick, sticky, cohesive dough forms. If it feels too wet to roll, fold in 1 to 2 tbsp extra almond flour.
3. **Chill 30 minutes:** Cover and refrigerate for **30 minutes** so the almond flour hydrates and the dough firms up for easy rolling.
4. **Roll in powdered sugar:** Preheat the oven to **350°F (175°C)** and line a baking sheet with parchment paper. Place the remaining 60 g powdered sugar in a small shallow bowl. Scoop rounded tablespoonfuls (about 18 g each), roll lightly between clean hands into smooth balls, and roll each ball generously in powdered sugar so it is thickly coated white.
5. **Bake until crackled and set:** Arrange the coated balls 1.5 inches (4 cm) apart on the parchment-lined tray and press the top of each very gently with a thumb so they slightly flatten. Bake on the center rack for **12 to 14 minutes**, until the tops crackle neatly and the bottoms turn pale golden—the centers will still feel very soft to the touch.
6. **Cool completely on the tray:** Let the snowballs cool undisturbed on the baking sheet for **10 minutes** before moving to a wire rack—they set into a fudgy, marzipan-soft chew as they cool.

## Recipe 2: Fudgy Vegan & Gluten-Free Chocolate-Hazelnut Crinkle Cookies

Rich dark chocolate meets toasted hazelnuts in a crackly, snow-dusted cookie that rivals any bakery brownie bite. Ground flaxseed replaces eggs effortlessly without changing the flavor.

- **Prep time:** 20 minutes (plus 45 minutes chilling)
- **Bake time:** 11 to 13 minutes
- **Total time:** about 1 hour 20 minutes
- **Yield:** about 24 crinkle cookies

### Ingredients

- 2 tbsp (14 g) finely ground flaxseed (flax meal) mixed with 5 tbsp (75 ml) warm water ("flax eggs")
- 1 cup (100 g) finely ground roasted hazelnuts (or blanched almond flour)
- 1 cup (100 g) certified gluten-free oat flour (or blend certified gluten-free rolled oats finely in a blender)
- 1/2 cup (45 g) unsweetened Dutch-process cocoa powder, sifted
- 1 tsp certified gluten-free baking powder
- 1/4 tsp fine sea salt
- 1/2 tsp ground Ceylon cinnamon or 1/2 tsp espresso powder (deepens chocolate flavor)
- 1/3 cup (80 ml) melted refined coconut oil (neutral flavor) or mild olive oil
- 3/4 cup (150 g) packed light brown sugar or coconut sugar
- 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
- 1/3 cup (55 g) vegan dark chocolate chips (optional, for melty pockets)
- **For rolling:** 1/3 cup (65 g) granulated sugar AND 1/2 cup (60 g) powdered sugar (in two separate small bowls—this double-roll prevents the white sugar from melting!)

### Instructions

1. **Make the flax eggs:** Whisk the 2 tbsp ground flaxseed and 5 tbsp warm water in a small cup and let sit **10 minutes** until thick and gel-like.
2. **Mix dry ingredients:** In a medium bowl, whisk together the ground hazelnuts, gluten-free oat flour, sifted cocoa powder, baking powder, salt and cinnamon/espresso powder.
3. **Whisk wet ingredients:** In a large bowl, whisk the melted (cooled slightly) coconut oil, brown sugar, vanilla extract and thickened flax mixture until smooth and glossy.
4. **Combine and chill:** Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture with a spatula until a thick, fudgy dough forms, then fold in the vegan dark chocolate chips if using. Cover and chill in the refrigerator for **45 minutes** (chilling is critical for distinct white crinkles).
5. **Double-roll for stark white crinkles:** Preheat the oven to **350°F (175°C)** and line two baking sheets with parchment paper. Scoop rounded tablespoonfuls (about 22 g) of chilled dough and roll into smooth balls. Roll each ball **first in granulated sugar** (this creates a moisture barrier), then **thickly in powdered sugar**. Space 2 inches (5 cm) apart on the trays.
6. **Bake until puffed and crackled:** Bake one tray at a time on the center rack for **11 to 13 minutes**, until puffed with dramatic dark fissures across the snowy tops and set edges (the centers should still look slightly glossy and soft). Cool on the baking sheet for 8 minutes before transferring to a wire rack.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Both doughs keep covered in the refrigerator for up to **48 hours** before scooping and baking. You can also freeze scooped, un-sugared dough balls for up to 2 months; roll in sugar and add 2 minutes to the bake time straight from frozen.
- **Storage:** Store completely cooled cookies in separate airtight tins layered with parchment paper at cool room temperature for **10 to 14 days**. Because both recipes are soft and chewy, don't store them in the same tin as crisp butter cookies—see our [Christmas cookie storage and baking timeline](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/).
- **Common mistake — skipping the double-roll on crinkle cookies:** Powdered sugar dissolves quickly into oil-based vegan doughs. Rolling first in granulated sugar keeps the outer powdered-sugar coat bright snow-white.
- **Common mistake — overbaking gluten-free nut cookies:** Without wheat gluten, nut-flour cookies feel very delicate hot from the oven and firm up dramatically while cooling. Pull them right at 12–13 minutes so they stay fudgy instead of crumbly.

## FAQ

### What other traditional Christmas cookies are naturally gluten-free?
Authentic [German Zimtsterne (Cinnamon Stars)](/en/blog/articles/german-zimtsterne-cinnamon-stars/), [Coconut Macaroons](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/), and flourless [Elisenlebkuchen](/en/blog/articles/german-lebkuchen-gingerbread-recipe/) baked directly on parchment paper (without wheat wafers) are all naturally gluten-free.

### Can I substitute almond flour in the Chocolate-Hazelnut Crinkle Cookies?
Yes—replace the 1 cup (100 g) ground hazelnuts 1:1 with blanched almond flour or finely ground walnuts, and the cookies bake identically.

### How can I make these cookies nut-free as well as vegan and gluten-free?
For a nut-free, vegan and gluten-free crinkle cookie, swap the 1 cup (100 g) ground hazelnuts for **1 cup (100 g) finely ground sunflower seeds or pumpkin seeds** (pulsed briefly in a food processor with 1 tbsp of the sugar so they don't turn to butter).

### Why didn't my gluten-free vegan cookies crackle nicely on top?
Usually either the dough wasn't chilled long enough (chilling 45 minutes creates the temperature contrast that makes the crust set and split as the center rises) or the baking powder was old and lost its lift.
