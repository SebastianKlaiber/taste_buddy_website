Choosing a Christmas dinner menu is less about finding the flashiest dishes on the internet and more about picking dishes that get along in one kitchen. A great menu balances rich and bright flavors, spreads work across oven, stovetop and make-ahead prep, and leaves you with enough energy to actually sit down with everyone.

Below are three complete Christmas dinner menu ideas for different cooking styles, followed by portion guidelines and a complete, reliable recipe for Garlic-Herb Crusted Beef Tenderloin with Red Wine Shallot Jus—one of the easiest showstopper mains because it cooks in under 45 minutes and frees the oven for sides.

## How to build a balanced Christmas dinner menu

Before picking recipes, check three things that make or break a holiday dinner:

1. **Cooking-method spread.** Never put four dishes in the oven at different temperatures at the same time. Pair an oven main with one stovetop side, one make-ahead braised or cold side, and one dessert you finished the day before.
2. **Texture and acid.** Holiday mains and gravies lean rich and savory. Balance them with something sharp or crisp: braised red cabbage with vinegar, a bitter-green salad with citrus vinaigrette, or tangy cranberry relish.
3. **One anchor recipe.** Pick one dish that carries the "special occasion" feeling—a whole roast goose, a prime tenderloin, or a puffed mushroom Wellington—and keep everything around it straightforward.

## Portion planning per guest

Scaling your menu accurately avoids panic at the grocery store and cuts down on fridge clutter on December 26.

| Menu Course | Amount per Adult | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soup starter | 1 cup (240 ml) | Keep starters light so guests have room for the main |
| Boneless roast (beef, pork, salmon) | 6 to 8 oz (170 to 225 g) raw | Order closer to 8 oz if you want sandwiches the next day |
| Bone-in poultry (goose, duck, turkey) | 1 to 1.5 lb (450 to 680 g) raw | Goose and duck have a higher bone-to-meat ratio than turkey |
| Vegetarian pastry main (Wellington/galette) | 1 generous slice (about 7 oz / 200 g) | Pair with a hearty lentil or chestnut filling |
| Starchy side (potatoes, dumplings, gratin) | 1/2 lb (225 g) raw potatoes | About 3/4 cup (180 g) cooked |
| Vegetable sides (combined) | 1 cup (150 to 200 g) total | Split across two contrasting vegetables |
| Sauce or gravy | 1/3 cup (80 ml) | Always make slightly more than you think you need |
| Dessert | 1 portion or 1/8 of a 9-inch cake | Plus a small plate of [Christmas cookies](/en/blog/articles/classic-christmas-cookie-recipes/) with coffee |

## Featured Recipe: Garlic-Herb Crusted Beef Tenderloin with Red Wine Shallot Jus

Beef tenderloin is forgiving as long as you use an instant-read thermometer and pull it early. Searing first builds a savory crust, and roasting at moderate heat keeps the interior evenly rosy from edge to center.

- **Prep time:** 25 minutes (plus 1 to 24 hours dry-brining, optional)
- **Cook time:** 35 to 45 minutes (plus 15 minutes resting)
- **Total time:** 1 hour 25 minutes
- **Servings:** 6 to 8

### Ingredients

**For the beef tenderloin:**
- 1 center-cut beef tenderloin roast, 3 to 3.5 lb (1.35 to 1.6 kg), trimmed and tied by the butcher
- 1 tbsp (15 g) kosher salt (or 1.5 tsp fine sea salt)
- 1.5 tsp freshly cracked black pepper
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) neutral high-heat oil (avocado or canola)
- 3 tbsp (42 g) unsalted butter, softened
- 2 tbsp (30 g) Dijon mustard
- 4 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 2 tbsp fresh rosemary, finely chopped
- 2 tbsp fresh thyme leaves, finely chopped

**For the red wine shallot jus:**
- 1 tbsp (14 g) unsalted butter, plus 2 tbsp (28 g) cold butter cubed for finishing
- 4 medium shallots (about 5 oz / 140 g), finely diced
- 1 cup (240 ml) dry red wine (Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot or Pinot Noir)
- 2 cups (480 ml) low-sodium beef stock
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

### Instructions

1. **Season ahead if possible:** Pat the tenderloin completely dry with paper towels. Season evenly all over with the kosher salt and black pepper. If you have time, set it uncovered on a wire rack over a rimmed baking sheet in the refrigerator for 4 to 24 hours—this seasons the meat deeply and helps it brown.
2. **Temper and preheat:** Remove the beef from the refrigerator 60 minutes before cooking so it cooks evenly. Preheat the oven to 400°F (200°C) with a rack in the center.
3. **Make the herb paste:** In a small bowl, mash together the 3 tbsp softened butter, Dijon mustard, minced garlic, rosemary and thyme until smooth.
4. **Sear the roast:** Heat the neutral oil in a large oven-safe skillet (cast iron or heavy stainless steel, at least 12 inches / 30 cm) over medium-high heat until shimmering. Sear the tenderloin for 2 minutes per side (all four sides, about 8 minutes total) until deeply browned. Transfer the roast to a plate briefly and pour off any scorched oil, leaving the browned bits in the pan.
5. **Coat and roast:** Spread the garlic-herb butter evenly over the top and sides of the seared beef. Set the tenderloin on a wire rack inside a roasting pan (or return it to a clean rimmed baking sheet) and roast for 22 to 32 minutes, until an instant-read thermometer inserted into the thickest center reads **125°F (52°C)** for medium-rare or **135°F (57°C)** for medium. Note that USDA recommends a minimum safe internal temperature of **145°F (63°C)** with a 3-minute rest for whole cuts of beef; pull at **140°F (60°C)** if you want to reach 145°F after carryover cooking.
6. **Rest:** Transfer the tenderloin to a warm cutting board, tent loosely with foil, and rest for 15 minutes. The internal temperature will rise by about 5°F to 8°F (3°C to 4°C) while resting, and the juices will redistribute.
7. **Make the red wine jus while it rests:** Place the searing skillet over medium heat and melt 1 tbsp butter. Add the diced shallots with a pinch of salt and cook for 4 minutes until soft and translucent. Pour in the red wine, scraping up the browned bits with a wooden spoon, and simmer briskly for 6 to 8 minutes until reduced by half.
8. **Finish the jus:** Add the beef stock, thyme sprigs and Worcestershire sauce. Simmer for 8 to 10 minutes until glossy and slightly syrupy (reduced to about 1.25 cups / 300 ml). Strain through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean saucepan, remove from the heat, and whisk in the 2 tbsp cold cubed butter a few pieces at a time until emulsified. Taste and adjust salt and pepper.
9. **Carve and serve:** Snip and remove the butcher's twine, slice the tenderloin into 3/4-inch (2 cm) medallions, and spoon the warm jus over the slices or pass it in a warmed gravy boat.

### Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Trim, tie and season the roast up to 24 hours ahead. You can also make the red wine jus (stopping right before whisking in the final cold butter) up to 2 days in advance; reheat gently and swirl in the cold butter right before serving.
- **Storage:** Cool leftover beef within 2 hours and store in an airtight container in the refrigerator for 3 to 4 days, following USDA leftover guidelines. Thinly sliced cold tenderloin makes excellent horseradish sandwiches on Boxing Day—find more ideas in our guide on [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — cooking by time alone:** Tenderloins vary widely in thickness. A long, thin roast reaches temperature 10 minutes faster than a short, thick one. Always check with a digital thermometer starting at 18 minutes.
- **Common mistake — skipping the tie:** Asking your butcher to fold and tie the narrow tail end under keeps the roast uniform in diameter so the ends don't overcook before the center is done.

## FAQ

### What is the best menu for Christmas dinner if I have a small oven?
Choose a quick-roasting main like beef tenderloin or duck breasts alongside stovetop sides (mashed potatoes, braised red cabbage, sautéed green beans) and a cold or room-temperature dessert. Only one dish needs the oven at a time.

### How many side dishes should I serve with Christmas dinner?
Three sides is the sweet spot: one starch (potatoes, dumplings or gratin), one rich or spiced vegetable (braised red cabbage or glazed carrots), and one fresh or green element (Brussels sprouts, green beans or a crisp salad).

### Can I cook a Christmas menu that works for both meat eaters and vegetarians?
Yes. Keep all side dishes—including braised cabbage, roasted vegetables and potatoes—vegetarian by using butter or olive oil and vegetable stock instead of meat drippings. Then serve one meat main and one focused vegetarian centerpiece like a mushroom Wellington.

### What temperature should beef tenderloin be cooked to?
For medium-rare, pull the roast at 125°F (52°C) so it rests to about 130–133°F (54–56°C). To meet the USDA safe minimum internal temperature for whole beef cuts, cook to 145°F (63°C) and rest for at least 3 minutes.
