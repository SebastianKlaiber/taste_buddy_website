Few holiday cookies deliver more pure butter-and-vanilla satisfaction than **German Spritzgebäck** (spritz butter cookies). Lightly crisp at the ridged edges, meltingly tender in the center, and half-dipped in glossy dark chocolate, they are the quintessential European bakery cookie you find on every Christmas coffee table.

Making great spritz butter cookies comes down to **dough consistency**: too cold and stiff, and you will split your piping bag (or jam a cookie press); too warm and greasy, and the pretty fluted ridges melt flat in the oven. This recipe hits the exact sweet spot—using **room-temperature butter whipped with powdered sugar**, a touch of ground almonds for tenderness, and a brief chill **after** piping so every ridge bakes razor-sharp.

## Piping bag vs. cookie press vs. meat grinder attachment

This versatile dough works with all three traditional tools—with one tiny adjustment:

- **Heavy-duty piping bag + large open star tip (1M or 10–12 mm star):** Use the recipe exactly as written below (adding 1 to 2 tbsp milk if your kitchen is cool so it pipes smoothly in S-shapes, rings and rosettes).
- **Cookie press (Spritz press):** Use **no extra milk** and press directly onto an **ungreased, unlined cool metal baking sheet**—the dough needs a slight grip on bare metal so the cookie releases cleanly from the press!
- **Traditional German meat grinder with *Spritzgebäck* attachment:** Chill the dough 30 minutes first so it extrudes in neat ribbons you slice into 2.5-inch (6 cm) bars or curve into S-shapes.

## Complete Recipe: Classic Chocolate-Dipped Spritz Butter Cookies (Spritzgebäck)

- **Prep time:** 30 minutes (plus 15 minutes chilling piped trays)
- **Bake time:** 10 to 12 minutes per tray
- **Total time:** about 1 hour 15 minutes
- **Yield:** about 45 to 50 cookies

### Ingredients

**For the spritz butter cookie dough:**
- 1 cup plus 1 tbsp (240 g) unsalted butter, **softened to cool room temperature** (about 65°F / 18°C—indentable with a finger, never melted or greasy)
- 1 cup plus 2 tbsp (140 g) powdered sugar (confectioners' sugar), sifted
- 1/4 tsp fine sea salt
- 2 tsp pure vanilla extract (or seeds from 1/2 vanilla bean)
- 1 tsp finely grated untreated lemon or orange zest (optional, brightens the butter)
- 2 large egg yolks (or 1 whole large egg—yolks give an extra tender, rich crumb)
- 2 cups plus 2 tbsp (260 g) all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup (50 g) finely ground blanched almonds (or swap 1:1 with 40 g cornstarch + 10 g flour for a nut-free classic)
- 1 to 2 tbsp (15 to 30 ml) whole milk or heavy cream (only if using a piping bag; omit for a cookie press)

**For the chocolate dip:**
- 7 oz (200 g) bittersweet or semi-sweet chocolate (55–70% cocoa), finely chopped
- 1 tsp neutral oil or coconut oil (keeps the chocolate glossy and smooth)
- Optional: 2 tbsp finely chopped pistachios or toasted hazelnuts for sprinkling

### Instructions

1. **Cream butter and sugar thoroughly:** In a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment (or with a hand mixer), beat the softened butter, sifted powdered sugar, salt, vanilla extract and citrus zest on medium speed for **3 to 4 minutes** until very pale, light and fluffy. Scrape down the sides of the bowl.
2. **Beat in egg yolks:** Add the egg yolks one at a time, beating well for 30 seconds after each addition until smooth and emulsified.
3. **Fold in dry ingredients:** Whisk the flour and finely ground almonds together in a bowl. Add them to the butter mixture in two additions on **low speed**, mixing just until no flour streaks remain. If using a piping bag and the dough feels too stiff to squeeze comfortably, mix in 1 to 2 tbsp milk until thick yet pipeable (like stiff buttercream).
4. **Pipe or press shapes:**
   - *Piping bag method:* Line two baking sheets with parchment paper. Fit a sturdy reusable canvas or heavy-duty disposable piping bag with a large open star tip (10–12 mm). Fill only half full (less hand pressure!), and pipe 2.5-inch (6 cm) S-shapes, small 2-inch (5 cm) rings, or neat rosettes spaced 1.5 inches (4 cm) apart.
   - *Cookie press method:* Leave two baking sheets completely **bare and ungreased** (no parchment!). Press shapes directly onto the cool metal sheets.
5. **Chill the shaped trays (the sharp-ridge secret!):** Slide the piped or pressed baking sheets into the refrigerator (or a cold garage/balcony) for **15 to 20 minutes** while the oven preheats to **350°F (175°C)**. Chilling *after* piping keeps the butter solid enough that every crisp star ridge stays defined in the oven!
6. **Bake until edges kiss golden:** Bake one tray at a time on the center rack for **10 to 12 minutes**, until the cookies are set, pale golden on top, and just lightly browned along the bottom rims. Let sit 3 minutes on the pan, then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely.
7. **Dip in melted chocolate:** Melt the chopped chocolate with 1 tsp oil gently over a double boiler (or in 20-second bursts in the microwave, stirring well between bursts). Dip one end—or half—of each completely cooled cookie into the chocolate, let excess drip off, and set on parchment paper. Sprinkle with chopped pistachios if desired. Let set at cool room temperature for 1 to 2 hours until the chocolate snaps cleanly.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Spritz cookies bake fast and keep exceptionally well—bake them **2 to 3 weeks before Christmas** alongside [classic butter cut-out cookies](/en/blog/articles/classic-christmas-cookie-recipes/) and [authentic Vanillekipferl](/en/blog/articles/authentic-vanillekipferl-recipe/).
- **Storage:** Layer completely dry, chocolate-set cookies between sheets of parchment paper in an airtight metal tin in a cool room (not above a warm radiator, or the chocolate softens). They stay crisp and buttery for **3 to 4 weeks**. Full tips on keeping crisp and soft cookies separate are in our [Christmas cookie storage guide](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/).
- **Food safety reminder:** Per FDA advice, skip tasting raw cookie dough because both unbaked flour and raw egg yolks can harbor pathogens.
- **Common mistake — using parchment with a cookie press:** Pressed dough won't stick to slick parchment or silicone mats, so it lifts right back up with the press. Always press onto bare, ungreased metal.
- **Common mistake — skipping the 15-minute post-piping chill:** Piping requires soft butter, which melts fast in a hot oven. A quick 15-minute fridge chill locks in the exact fluted pattern.
- **Common mistake — overfilling a thin piping bag:** Overstuffed disposable bags burst right at the seam. Fill only halfway and use a sturdy cloth or thick 18-inch piping bag.

## FAQ

### Why did my spritz butter cookies flatten out in the oven?
Either the butter was melted/too warm when mixed, or the trays went straight into a hot oven without chilling. Chilling the shaped cookies on the baking sheet for 15 minutes right before baking solves flattening immediately.

### Can I make nut-free spritz butter cookies?
Yes! Replace the 1/2 cup (50 g) ground almonds with **1/3 cup (40 g) cornstarch plus 1 tbsp (10 g) all-purpose flour**. Cornstarch keeps the crumb ultra-tender and melt-in-your-mouth without nuts.

### What if I don't own a cookie press or piping bag?
Roll the chilled dough into 1-inch (2.5 cm) balls, place on a parchment-lined sheet, press each gently with the tines of a fork to create crisp ridges, chill 10 minutes, and bake 11 to 13 minutes—same buttery flavor, zero special gear.

### Can I freeze baked spritz butter cookies?
Yes, plain un-dipped spritz cookies freeze cleanly in an airtight container for up to **2 months**. Thaw uncovered at room temperature for 1 hour so condensation doesn't soften them, then dip in fresh melted chocolate before serving.
