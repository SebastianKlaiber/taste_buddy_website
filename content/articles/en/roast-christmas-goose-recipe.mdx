A roast Christmas goose has a reputation for being intimidating, mostly because people treat it like a turkey. Goose is completely different: it is all dark, deeply flavorful meat wrapped in a thick subcutaneous layer of fat. If you roast it dry and hot like a lean turkey breast, the kitchen fills with smoke and the legs stay chewy. If you prick the skin, render the fat patiently, and finish with high heat, you get mahogany, crackly skin, tender meat, and jars of liquid-gold goose fat for roasting potatoes all winter.

This traditional roast Christmas goose recipe uses a gentle roasting temperature first so the legs turn fork-tender, followed by a crisp-skin finish and a rich, skimmed pan gravy built from the giblets and aromatics.

## Buying, thawing and sizing a Christmas goose

- **Size per person:** Goose has more bone and fat than turkey. Plan **1.25 to 1.5 lb (570 to 680 g) raw weight per adult**. A **10 to 11 lb (4.5 to 5 kg)** goose comfortably feeds **6 people** (or 8 if you serve a starter and hearty sides).
- **Safe thawing:** Most geese outside specialty farms are sold frozen. According to the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, allow **24 hours in the refrigerator for every 4 to 5 pounds** of bird. A 10 to 11 lb goose needs **2.5 to 3 days** to thaw completely in a tray on the bottom fridge shelf. Never thaw a goose on the kitchen counter.
- **Save the fat:** A single goose renders 2 to 3 cups (480 to 720 ml) of pure fat. Spoon it off during roasting, strain it into clean glass jars, and refrigerate—it keeps for months and makes unmatched roast potatoes.

## Complete Recipe: Traditional Roast Christmas Goose with Apple-Onion Stuffing

- **Prep time:** 40 minutes (plus overnight air-drying in the fridge, recommended)
- **Cook time:** 3 hours 15 minutes to 3 hours 45 minutes (plus 25 minutes resting)
- **Total time:** about 4 hours 45 minutes
- **Servings:** 6

### Ingredients

**For the goose:**
- 1 whole goose, 10 to 11 lb (4.5 to 5 kg), thawed if frozen, giblets and neck reserved
- 2 tbsp (30 g) kosher salt (or 1 tbsp fine sea salt)
- 2 tsp freshly cracked black pepper
- 1 tbsp dried mugwort (Beifuß) or dried marjoram, crumbled

**For the aromatic cavity stuffing:**
- 3 tart, firm apples (such as Braeburn, Granny Smith or Boskoop), cored and quartered
- 2 medium yellow onions, peeled and quartered
- 1 untreated orange, scrubbed and quartered
- 1/2 cup (85 g) pitted prunes (optional, adds subtle sweetness to the gravy)
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme or 2 sprigs mugwort

**For the roasting pan and gravy:**
- 2 carrots, coarsely chopped
- 2 stalks celery (or 4 oz / 115 g celeriac), coarsely chopped
- 1 large onion, quartered
- 2 cups (480 ml) water or chicken/poultry stock, plus 1.5 cups (360 ml) more stock for the gravy
- 1 cup (240 ml) dry red wine or apple cider
- 2 tbsp (16 g) all-purpose flour or 1.5 tbsp (12 g) cornstarch mixed with 2 tbsp cold water
- 1 tbsp red currant jelly or cranberry sauce (optional, to balance the gravy)
- 1 tsp salt dissolved in 1/4 cup (60 ml) cold water (for brushing the skin at the end)

### Instructions

1. **Prep and air-dry the goose (ideally the night before):** Remove the neck, giblets and any loose pads of fat inside the body cavity. Rinse only if needed—USDA advises against washing raw poultry to avoid splashing bacteria around the sink—and instead pat the goose thoroughly dry inside and out with paper towels.
2. **Prick the skin carefully:** Using a sharp wooden skewer or the tip of a paring knife, prick the skin all over the breasts, thighs and back at 1-inch (2.5 cm) intervals. Go at a shallow angle so you pierce the skin and fat layer **without** poking into the meat underneath.
3. **Season:** Rub the cavity with half the salt, pepper and dried marjoram/mugwort. Rub the outside skin with the remaining salt and pepper. If you have time, set the goose uncovered on a rack in the fridge overnight; dry skin crisps dramatically better.
4. **Stuff and truss:** Remove the goose from the fridge 1 hour before roasting. Preheat the oven to **325°F (165°C)**. Toss the quartered apples, onions, orange, prunes and herb sprigs together and pack them loosely into the cavity. Close the opening with metal skewers or kitchen twine and tie the legs loosely together. Tuck the wing tips under the back.
5. **Set up the roasting pan:** Place the chopped carrots, celery, onion, reserved neck and giblets (skip the liver, which can turn gravy bitter) in the bottom of a large, deep roasting pan. Pour in 2 cups (480 ml) water or stock. Set a sturdy V-rack or flat roasting rack on top, and place the goose **breast-side down** on the rack.
6. **Roast breast-side down (1.5 hours):** Roast uncovered at 325°F (165°C) for 90 minutes. Every 35 to 40 minutes, tilt the pan and spoon or bulb-baste out the accumulated rendered fat into a heatproof bowl (this prevents smoking and splattering). Add a splash of hot water if the pan looks dry.
7. **Flip breast-side up (1.5 to 2 hours):** Carefully turn the goose breast-side up using folded clean kitchen towels or silicone mitts. Prick any pockets of fat around the thighs again and continue roasting at 325°F (165°C) for another 90 to 110 minutes, ladling off excess fat every 40 minutes.
8. **Check internal temperature:** Insert an instant-read thermometer into the thickest part of the breast, the innermost part of the thigh, and the innermost part of the wing (without touching bone). Per USDA food-safety guidelines, all parts must reach at least **165°F (74°C)**. Goose legs are even more tender around **175°F to 180°F (79°C to 82°C)**.
9. **Crisp the skin:** Remove the goose from the oven, transfer it on its rack to a clean baking sheet (so the pan drippings don't burn), and increase the oven temperature to **425°F (220°C)**. Brush the skin lightly with the salted water—do not brush with fat, which softens skin. Return the goose to the oven for **10 to 15 minutes**, watching closely, until the skin is deep golden brown and blistered.
10. **Rest and make the gravy:** Transfer the goose to a carving board and rest uncovered for **20 to 25 minutes** (covering tightly with foil steams the crispy skin soft). Meanwhile, strain the contents of the roasting pan through a fine-mesh sieve into a fat separator or tall pitcher, pressing the softened vegetables to extract flavor. Let settle for 5 minutes, then pour off the clear fat on top, reserving the dark savory juices underneath.
11. **Finish the gravy:** Place a saucepan over medium heat, add 2 tbsp of the reserved goose fat and whisk in the flour (or skip fat and thicken with a cornstarch slurry at the end) for 2 minutes. Deglaze with the red wine or apple cider, simmer 3 minutes, then whisk in the skimmed pan juices and 1.5 cups (360 ml) stock. Simmer 8 to 10 minutes until velvety. Stir in the red currant jelly if using, season to taste, and carve the goose to serve.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** You can roast the goose completely the day before, through step 8, until every part reads at least 165°F (74°C). Skip only the final crisping step. Carve it, refrigerate the pieces in shallow containers within 2 hours, and on Christmas Day crisp them in a 425°F (220°C) oven for 20 to 25 minutes until heated through to 165°F (74°C). Never stop roasting early and finish the next day: USDA advises against partially cooking poultry, because the half-cooked meat can let bacteria grow. The gravy is even better made a day ahead because chilled fat lifts off the top in a solid disk.
- **Storage and leftovers:** Remove all meat from the carcass and refrigerate meat, stuffing and gravy in shallow containers within 2 hours of serving. Eat within 3 to 4 days (USDA guideline) or freeze. Simmer the carcass with an onion and bay leaf for rich goose soup—more ideas in [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — eating the cavity stuffing safely:** Cavity aromatics flavor the bird and juices beautifully, but because they absorb raw poultry juices, USDA advises checking that any stuffing you intend to eat reaches **165°F (74°C)** in the center. If you want traditional bread stuffing, bake it in a separate casserole dish and discard the cavity apples and onions.
- **Common mistake — basting with goose fat:** Unlike turkey, goose self-bastes from within. Basting the outside with fat actually slows crisping; salted water or lightly salted beer gives a far better crunch.

## FAQ

### How long do you roast a 10-pound Christmas goose?
At 325°F (165°C), a 10 lb (4.5 kg) goose takes about 3 to 3.5 hours total, roughly 18 to 20 minutes per pound, plus 10 to 15 minutes at 425°F (220°C) to crisp the skin. Always confirm with a thermometer (at least 165°F / 74°C in breast, thigh and wing).

### Why do you start roasting a goose breast-side down?
Starting breast-side down lets fat from the back render out while protecting the leaner breast meat from drying out. Flipping it breast-side up for the second half renders the breast skin cleanly so it crisps evenly at the end.

### How do I keep goose skin crispy while it rests?
Rest the goose uncovered on a warm board in a draft-free spot for 20 to 25 minutes. Covering it tightly with aluminum foil traps steam and softens the crisp skin within minutes.

### What can I do with leftover rendered goose fat?
Strain it through a fine sieve into clean glass jars and store in the refrigerator for up to 3 months (or freeze for up to a year). Use it to roast potatoes, sear Brussels sprouts, or sauté braised red cabbage.
