If a whole Christmas goose feels too big for your table, roast duck and roast turkey are the two best holiday poultry alternatives—each solving a different hosting problem. A whole roast duck is ideal for **2 to 4 guests** (or two ducks for 6 to 8), offering rich dark meat and shattering crispy skin in under two and a half hours. Roast turkey scales effortlessly for **8 to 14 guests**, gives you light and dark meat on the same platter, and leaves plenty for Boxing Day sandwiches.

Below you'll find a complete, foolproof Christmas roast duck recipe with orange-thyme glaze and pan sauce, plus a side-by-side guide and herb-butter method for roasting a Christmas turkey without dry breast meat.

## Duck vs. turkey for Christmas dinner

| Factor | Whole Roast Duck | Whole Roast Turkey |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical weight | 5 to 5.5 lb (2.2 to 2.5 kg) | 12 to 16 lb (5.4 to 7.2 kg) |
| Servings per bird | 3 to 4 adults | 10 to 14 adults |
| Meat profile | Rich, all dark meat with thick fat layer | Lean white breast meat + darker thighs/legs |
| Roasting strategy | Slow render first, high heat to crisp | Moderate steady heat, dry-brine or herb butter |
| Oven time | 2 hours 15 min to 2 hours 30 min | 2 hours 45 min to 4 hours (un-stuffed) |
| USDA safe minimum temp | 165°F (74°C) in breast, thigh & wing | 165°F (74°C) in breast, thigh & wing |
| Fridge thawing time (USDA) | About 1 to 1.5 days (24h per 4–5 lb) | 3 to 4 days (24h per 4–5 lb) |

If you're feeding 6 to 8 people and love duck, roasting **two 5 lb (2.2 kg) ducks side by side** on one large sheet pan rack takes the exact same oven time as one duck and gives everyone both breast and leg meat.

## Complete Recipe: Crispy Christmas Roast Duck with Orange-Thyme Glaze

Duck needs the same fat-rendering trick as our [roast Christmas goose recipe](/en/blog/articles/roast-christmas-goose-recipe/), just on a faster clock. Scoring or pricking the skin and starting at a gentle 325°F (165°C) melts out the excess fat without smoking up the house, and a sticky orange-honey glaze in the last 15 minutes turns the skin lacquered and crisp.

- **Prep time:** 25 minutes (plus optional overnight uncovered chill in the fridge)
- **Cook time:** 2 hours 15 minutes (plus 15 minutes resting)
- **Total time:** 2 hours 55 minutes
- **Servings:** 4

### Ingredients

**For the roast duck:**
- 1 whole Pekin duck, 5 to 5.5 lb (2.2 to 2.5 kg), thawed if frozen, giblets and neck removed
- 1 tbsp (15 g) kosher salt (or 1.5 tsp fine sea salt)
- 1 tsp freshly cracked black pepper
- 1 tsp dried marjoram or thyme
- 1 untreated orange, scrubbed and quartered
- 1 tart apple (Granny Smith or Honeycrisp), cored and quartered
- 1 small yellow onion, peeled and quartered
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 cinnamon stick

**For the orange-thyme glaze:**
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) freshly squeezed orange juice
- 2 tbsp (42 g) honey or maple syrup
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) balsamic vinegar or apple cider vinegar
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp grated fresh orange zest

**For the pan gravy:**
- 1 cup (240 ml) low-sodium chicken or duck stock
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) dry red wine or Ruby Port
- 1 tbsp (8 g) cornstarch whisked with 2 tbsp cold water
- Salt and pepper, to taste

### Instructions

1. **Dry and prick the skin:** Remove the neck and giblet packet from the cavity and discard any large loose fat pads near the tail. Pat the duck thoroughly dry inside and out with paper towels—avoid rinsing raw poultry under the tap, as USDA notes splashing spreads bacteria across sinks and counters. Using a sharp skewer or the tip of a paring knife, prick the skin at 1-inch (2.5 cm) intervals all over the breast, thighs and back at a shallow angle, piercing skin and fat without cutting into the meat.
2. **Season:** Mix the kosher salt, black pepper and dried marjoram. Rub half inside the cavity and half over the exterior skin. For the crispiest possible skin, place the duck uncovered on a rack over a rimmed baking sheet in the fridge for 8 to 24 hours.
3. **Stuff aromatics and preheat:** Remove the duck from the fridge 45 minutes before roasting. Preheat the oven to **325°F (165°C)**. Tuck the quartered orange, apple, onion, thyme sprigs and cinnamon stick loosely inside the cavity. Fold the wing tips under the back and tie the legs loosely with kitchen twine.
4. **First roast — breast-side up on a rack:** Set the duck breast-side up on a wire rack inside a deep roasting pan. Pour 1/2 cup (120 ml) hot water into the bottom of the pan (this stops rendered fat from smoking). Roast for **1 hour**.
5. **Flip and drain fat:** Carefully tilt the pan and spoon or bulb-baste out the rendered duck fat into a heatproof jar (save it for roasting potatoes). Flip the duck **breast-side down** and roast for **45 minutes**.
6. **Make the glaze:** While the duck roasts, combine the orange juice, honey, balsamic vinegar, Dijon mustard and orange zest in a small saucepan. Simmer over medium heat for 6 to 8 minutes until reduced by half to a light syrup.
7. **Flip back, glaze and crisp:** Flip the duck **breast-side up** again, pour off any accumulated fat, and increase the oven temperature to **425°F (220°C)**. Brush half the glaze over the skin and roast for **10 minutes**. Brush with the remaining glaze and roast another **5 to 10 minutes**, watching carefully so the honey doesn't scorch, until the skin is deeply mahogany and crisp.
8. **Verify USDA temperature:** Check with an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part of the breast, innermost thigh and innermost wing (avoiding bone). All areas must reach at least **165°F (74°C)**; duck legs are even more tender around **175°F (79°C)**.
9. **Rest and make quick pan sauce:** Transfer the duck to a carving board and rest uncovered for **15 minutes**. Pour the roasting pan liquid into a fat separator, skim off the clear fat on top, and pour the dark savory pan juices into the glaze saucepan. Add the stock and red wine or Port, simmer 5 minutes, whisk in the cornstarch slurry, and simmer 2 more minutes until glossy. Season to taste, carve the duck into quarters, and serve.

## Roasting a Christmas turkey without drying it out

If you're cooking for a crowd of 8 to 14, a **12 to 14 lb (5.4 to 6.4 kg) whole turkey** is the easiest way to feed everyone at once. Use these exact principles to keep the breast juicy while the thighs cook completely:

- **Dry-brine 24 to 48 hours ahead:** Rub the turkey all over (and under the breast skin) with **1/2 tsp kosher salt per pound** (about 2 tbsp / 30 g for a 12 lb bird) plus 1 tsp baking powder (optional, aids browning). Leave uncovered in the fridge on a rimmed tray.
- **Herb compound butter:** Mash **1/2 cup (115 g) softened unsalted butter** with 2 tbsp chopped sage and thyme, 2 minced garlic cloves, 1 tsp lemon zest and 1/2 tsp black pepper. Slide two-thirds of the butter directly under the breast skin and rub the rest outside.
- **Roasting temperature and timing:** Roast an un-stuffed 12 to 14 lb turkey on a rack at **350°F (175°C)** for roughly **2 hours 45 minutes to 3 hours 15 minutes** (about 13 to 15 minutes per pound). Tent the breast loosely with foil if it browns deeply before the last 45 minutes.
- **USDA safe temperature:** Pull the turkey only when an instant-read thermometer reads at least **165°F (74°C)** in the innermost part of the thigh, the innermost part of the wing, and the thickest part of the breast. Rest uncovered for **30 to 40 minutes**—this is the exact window to bake your [classic Christmas side dishes](/en/blog/articles/classic-christmas-side-dishes/).

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Both duck and turkey benefit massively from overnight dry-brining. You can also simmer the duck glaze and turkey herb butter up to 3 days ahead and keep them covered in the fridge. For full multi-day prep, check our [make-ahead Christmas dinner guide](/en/blog/articles/make-ahead-christmas-dinner/) and [holiday dinner planning checklist](/en/blog/articles/holiday-dinner-planning-checklist/).
- **Storage:** Carve all leftover meat off the bone within 2 hours of serving and refrigerate in shallow containers. Per USDA guidelines, eat within **3 to 4 days** or freeze. Turn duck or turkey carcasses into rich winter broth—see [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — stuffing a turkey tightly with bread:** Dense stuffing inside a large bird slows heat transfer, meaning the breast overcooks long before the stuffing center hits the USDA-required **165°F (74°C)**. Bake bread stuffing in a buttered casserole dish instead and put only loose aromatics (onion, lemon, herbs) in the cavity.
- **Common mistake — glazing duck too early:** Honey or sugar glazes burn quickly over a two-hour roast. Apply glaze only in the final 15 minutes.

## FAQ

### How many people does one 5-pound roast duck feed?
One 5 to 5.5 lb (2.2 to 2.5 kg) whole duck yields 2 breasts and 2 leg quarters, comfortably serving 4 adults when paired with classic holiday sides like red cabbage, potatoes and a starter.

### How do I make Christmas roast duck skin really crispy?
Three steps make the biggest difference: patting the skin dry and air-drying it uncovered in the fridge overnight, pricking the skin and fat layer (without piercing the meat) so fat drains during roasting, and finishing at 425°F (220°C) for the last 15 minutes.

### Can I roast two ducks in the oven at the same time?
Yes. Place two 5 lb ducks side by side on a large rack over a rimmed roasting pan with at least 2 inches (5 cm) of space between them for air circulation. The total roasting time stays roughly the same—just check each bird with a thermometer.

### How long does a frozen turkey or duck take to thaw in the fridge?
The USDA recommends 24 hours of refrigerator thawing for every 4 to 5 pounds (1.8 to 2.3 kg) of poultry. A 5 lb duck takes about 1 to 1.5 days, while a 14 lb turkey needs 3 to 4 full days on the bottom shelf.
