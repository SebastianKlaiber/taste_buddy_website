In Switzerland, Germany and France, raclette and cheese fondue are two of the most beloved Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve traditions—and once you host one, it is easy to see why. Everything gets sliced and prepped in bowls hours before guests arrive, nobody is stuck in the kitchen checking a roast, meat eaters and vegetarians build their own exact plates, and dinner stretches happily over two or three hours of conversation.

Here is everything you need to host a memorable tabletop Christmas meal: exact per-person shopping math, 8 creative Christmas raclette pan combinations, food-safety pointers for raw grill toppings, and a complete, silky **Classic Swiss Cheese Fondue (Moitié-Moitié) Recipe** that never splits.

## How much raclette cheese and toppings per person?

Running out of melting cheese—or buying triple the potatoes—is the only way raclette goes wrong. Use these realistic per-adult ratios:

| Ingredient | Amount per Adult | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raclette cheese (sliced 1/4-inch / 5 mm) | 7 to 9 oz (200 to 250 g) | Buy traditional Swiss or French Raclette cheese; add 1 alternative like Gouda, Camembert or vegan melting slices |
| Small waxy potatoes (boiled in skins) | 7 to 9 oz (200 to 250 g) | Keep warm in a linen-lined basket or insulated pot |
| Meat or fish for the top grill (optional) | 4 to 5 oz (120 to 150 g) | Thin strips of beef sirloin, chicken breast, shrimp, or prosciutto |
| Vegetables & mushrooms | 5 to 6 oz (150 to 175 g) | Mushrooms, bell peppers, zucchini, broccoli florets (lightly blanched), cherry tomatoes |
| Pickles & tangy accents | 2 to 3 oz (60 to 80 g) | Cornichons, pickled pearl onions, olives, jalapeños |
| Crusty baguette | 3 to 4 slices (3 oz / 80 g) | Essential for soaking up cheese and dipping |

## 8 creative Christmas raclette pan ideas

Put a small printed card on the table with combination ideas so guests move beyond plain cheese-and-potatoes:

1. **The Alpine Classic:** Sliced boiled baby potatoes, crispy bacon or smoked ham, pickled pearl onions, raclette cheese, cracked black pepper and smoked paprika.
2. **Pear, Walnut & Gorgonzola (Vegetarian):** Thin slices of ripe pear, toasted walnuts, a pinch of fresh thyme, and creamy Gorgonzola or raclette cheese—finished with a drizzle of honey after melting.
3. **Garlic-Herb Mushroom Melt (Vegetarian):** Pre-sliced cremini or shiitake mushrooms seared briefly on the top grill with garlic butter, moved into the pan with thyme and raclette cheese.
4. **Christmas Cranberry & Brie:** Soft Brie or Camembert melted over toasted baguette cubes with a spoonful of cranberry sauce and chopped pecans.
5. **Mediterranean:** Roasted red peppers, black olives, cherry tomatoes, red onion and feta or raclette cheese, finished with oregano.
6. **Smoked Salmon & Crème Fraîche (Flammkuchen style):** Crisp a tiny tortilla or thin flatbread triangle in the pan, top with crème fraîche, red onion, melted mild cheese, and drape cold smoked salmon and fresh dill on top *after* pulling it from the grill.
7. **Steakhouse Pan:** Thin beef strips seared hot on the top plate, tucked into the pan with sautéed onions, cornichons and raclette cheese.
8. **Sweet Dessert Pan:** Sliced banana or apple with a pinch of cinnamon, dark chocolate chunks and mini marshmallows or toasted hazelnuts—wipe the pan clean first.

## Complete Recipe: Classic Swiss Cheese Fondue (Moitié-Moitié)

*Moitié-Moitié* ("half and half") combines nutty Gruyère with creamy Vacherin Fribourgeois (or Fontina/Emmental if Vacherin is unavailable). Tossing the grated cheese in cornstarch before melting guarantees a velvety, clump-free pot every time.

- **Prep time:** 20 minutes
- **Cook time:** 15 minutes
- **Total time:** 35 minutes
- **Servings:** 4 (double easily for 8 using two fondue pots)

### Ingredients

- 1 clove garlic, halved
- 14 oz (400 g) aged Swiss Gruyère cheese, coarsely grated (rinds removed)
- 14 oz (400 g) Vacherin Fribourgeois cheese (or Swiss Emmental / Italian Fontina), coarsely grated
- 1.5 tbsp (12 g) cornstarch
- 1.25 cups (300 ml) dry white wine (such as Swiss Chasselas, Sauvignon Blanc or dry Pinot Grigio)
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) fresh lemon juice (acid prevents the cheese proteins from clumping)
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) Kirschwasser (cherry brandy, optional—or replace with 2 tbsp extra white wine)
- 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground white or black pepper

**For dipping:**
- 1 large day-old crusty baguette or country sourdough loaf (about 16 oz / 450 g), cut into 1-inch (2.5 cm) cubes, each with a bit of crust
- 1.5 lb (680 g) small waxy potatoes, boiled until tender
- Steamed broccoli florets, crisp apple wedges, roasted mushrooms, cornichons and pickled pearl onions

### Instructions

1. **Prep the pot and cheese:** Rub the inside bottom and walls of a heavy ceramic or enameled cast-iron fondue pot (*caquelon*) firmly with the cut sides of the garlic clove, then discard the garlic (or mince it finely and leave it in if you love bold garlic). In a large bowl, toss both grated cheeses evenly with the cornstarch so every shred is lightly coated.
2. **Warm the wine:** Pour the white wine and lemon juice into the fondue pot and heat over medium-low heat on the stovetop until tiny bubbles begin to form around the edges—do **not** bring it to a rolling boil.
3. **Melt gradually in a figure-eight:** Add the cheese one generous handful at a time, stirring constantly in a slow figure-eight motion with a wooden spoon. Wait until each handful is nearly melted before adding the next. Keep the heat low so the mixture barely simmers.
4. **Finish and season:** Once all the cheese is melted into a smooth, glossy ribbon, stir in the Kirschwasser (if using), nutmeg and freshly ground pepper. Taste and adjust pepper (aged Gruyère is already salty, so extra salt is rarely needed).
5. **Move to the burner and serve:** Transfer the pot carefully to the lit tabletop fondue burner set to a low flame so it stays gently warm without scorching the bottom. Encourage guests to stir the bottom in a figure-eight whenever they dip a bread cube!

## Make-ahead, food safety and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Chop all raclette vegetables, pickles, fruit, bread cubes and sauces **4 to 6 hours ahead**, arrange them in small bowls, cover and refrigerate. Boil the small potatoes 1 hour before guests arrive so they stay steaming hot. For fondue, grate the cheese and toss it with cornstarch up to 24 hours ahead in a sealed bag.
- **Raw meat safety on a raclette grill:** If serving raw beef, pork or chicken for the top grill plate, keep raw meat on a separate platter with its own dedicated tongs—never use the same fork you eat with to handle raw meat. Per USDA guidelines, cook poultry strips to **165°F (74°C)** and beef/pork thoroughly until no pink remains (thin 1/4-inch slices cook through quickly on a fully preheated plate).
- **Storage and leftovers:** Refrigerate unused toppings and leftover cheese within 2 hours of finishing dinner (eat cooked leftovers within 3 to 4 days per USDA). Leftover raclette cheese melts wonderfully into next-day grilled cheese, pasta bake or frittata—more ideas in [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — overheating cheese fondue:** Boiling fondue breaks the emulsion and leaves oily puddles and rubbery strands. If it ever starts to separate, remove from heat, whisk in 1 tsp lemon juice plus 1 tsp cornstarch dissolved in 1 tbsp white wine, and stir vigorously over very low heat until smooth again.
- **Common mistake — skipping ventilation:** Raclette cheese aroma lingers! Crack a window slightly after dinner and simmer a small pot of water with cinnamon sticks and orange peel—or serve homemade [mulled wine](/en/blog/articles/mulled-wine-christmas-drinks-recipes/).

## FAQ

### What is the best cheese for Christmas raclette?
Authentic Swiss or French Raclette cheese melts smoothly without turning oily. For variety, add a second platter of aged Gouda, Gruyère, Fontina, Brie or Gorgonzola, plus a good plant-based melting slice for vegan guests.

### Can you make cheese fondue without alcohol?
Yes. Replace the 1.25 cups (300 ml) dry white wine and Kirschwasser with **1.25 cups (300 ml) dry apple cider or light vegetable broth** plus **1.5 tbsp fresh lemon juice**—the acidity is what keeps the melted cheese silky and lump-free.

### What drinks pair best with raclette and cheese fondue?
Dry, crisp white wine (like Swiss Chasselas, Riesling or Sauvignon Blanc) or hot unsweetened black or herbal tea cut through the rich melted cheese best. Avoid very icy sugary drinks, which can make rich melted cheese feel heavy.

### How do I make Christmas raclette work for vegetarian guests?
Keep raw meat on a separate half of the top grill (or skip raw meat entirely) and focus on hearty grillables like marinated king oyster mushrooms, halloumi cubes, zucchini ribbons, pre-cooked baby potatoes, roasted sweet potato slices and caramelized onions.
