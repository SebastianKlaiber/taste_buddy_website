A holiday main dish gets the applause when it hits the table, but the Christmas side dishes are what people go back for seconds of. The best holiday sides balance a rich roast with three distinct roles: one **sweet-sour vegetable** to cut through richness, one **gravy-catching starch**, and one **crisp or bright green vegetable** for texture.

This guide gives you three classic, reliable Christmas side dish recipes that pair with [roast Christmas goose](/en/blog/articles/roast-christmas-goose-recipe/), [roast duck or turkey](/en/blog/articles/christmas-roast-duck-and-turkey/), beef tenderloin, or a [vegetarian Mushroom Wellington](/en/blog/articles/vegetarian-vegan-christmas-mains/)—along with how to time them around a single oven.

## How to balance your Christmas side dishes

When choosing sides for 6 to 8 guests, aim for **three sides** rather than six:

1. **Make-ahead braised vegetable (stovetop):** Spiced red cabbage with apples actually tastes *better* cooked 1 to 2 days in advance and reheated gently on the stove.
2. **Primary starch (stovetop or oven during the roast's rest):** Potato dumplings simmer on the stove while the oven stays free, or crispy roast potatoes go into a hot oven the moment the roast comes out to rest.
3. **Quick green contrast (10 to 15 minutes):** Blanched green beans tossed with toasted almonds, or shaved Brussels sprouts sautéed in a wide skillet right before serving.

For exact per-person quantities and a full oven schedule, check our [holiday dinner planning checklist](/en/blog/articles/holiday-dinner-planning-checklist/) and [Christmas dinner menu ideas](/en/blog/articles/christmas-dinner-menu-ideas/).

## Recipe 1: Spiced Braised Red Cabbage with Apples (Rotkohl)

This traditional German-style braised red cabbage is sweet, tangy, warmly spiced, and completely vegetarian (and vegan if you use oil instead of butter).

- **Prep time:** 20 minutes
- **Cook time:** 1 hour 15 minutes
- **Total time:** 1 hour 35 minutes
- **Servings:** 6 to 8

### Ingredients

- 1 medium head red cabbage, about 2.2 lb (1 kg), outer leaves removed, quartered, cored and finely shredded
- 2 tbsp (28 g) unsalted butter, neutral oil, or rendered goose/duck fat
- 1 large yellow onion (about 7 oz / 200 g), finely diced
- 2 tart, firm apples (Granny Smith or Braeburn), peeled, cored and diced
- 3 tbsp (45 ml) apple cider vinegar (plus 1 tbsp more at the end if needed)
- 2 tbsp (25 g) brown sugar
- 1 cup (240 ml) apple cider or dry red wine
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) vegetable broth or water
- 2 bay leaves
- 4 whole cloves
- 4 juniper berries, lightly crushed (optional)
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 1.5 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp red currant jelly or cranberry sauce (for finishing)

### Instructions

1. **Sauté the onions:** Melt the butter or heat the oil in a large Dutch oven or heavy pot over medium heat. Add the diced onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 5 minutes until translucent.
2. **Wilt the cabbage and apples:** Add the shredded red cabbage and diced apples, tossing well for 4 to 5 minutes until the cabbage softens slightly and deepens in color. Sprinkle in the brown sugar and pour in the apple cider vinegar—the acid immediately locks in the vibrant purple-red hue.
3. **Add liquids and spices:** Tie the cloves, juniper berries and bay leaves in a tea infuser or small square of cheesecloth (so they are easy to remove later). Add them to the pot along with the cinnamon stick, apple cider or red wine, broth, salt and pepper.
4. **Braise low and slow:** Bring to a gentle simmer, cover tightly with a lid, reduce heat to low, and braise for **60 to 70 minutes**, stirring every 20 minutes. Add a splash of water if the bottom ever looks dry.
5. **Finish and adjust balance:** Remove the lid, fish out the spice bundle and cinnamon stick, and cook uncovered for 5 minutes if excess liquid remains. Stir in the red currant jelly, then taste carefully—adjust with an extra splash of vinegar for brightness or a pinch of salt.

## Recipe 2: Classic German Potato Dumplings (Kartoffelklöße)

Soft, springy potato dumplings are the ultimate vehicle for holiday gravy. This half-cooked, half-mashed style skips the tricky raw-grating step while staying light and pillowy—never gummy.

- **Prep time:** 25 minutes (plus 15 minutes cooling)
- **Cook time:** 45 minutes total (boiling potatoes + simmering dumplings)
- **Total time:** 1 hour 25 minutes
- **Servings:** 6 (12 dumplings)

### Ingredients

- 2.2 lb (1 kg) starchy, floury potatoes (such as Russet or Idaho)
- 1/2 cup plus 2 tbsp (100 g) potato starch (or cornstarch, though potato starch gives the best springy texture)
- 2 large egg yolks
- 2 tbsp (28 g) unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled
- 1.5 tsp kosher salt, plus 2 tbsp salt for the simmering water
- 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
- Optional center crunch: 1 cup (40 g) small bread cubes toasted golden in 1 tbsp (14 g) butter

### Instructions

1. **Boil potatoes in their skins:** Scrub the potatoes and place them whole and unpeeled in a large pot covered with cold water. Bring to a boil and simmer for **25 to 30 minutes** until easily pierced with a knife. Boiling whole with skins on prevents them from absorbing excess water.
2. **Peel and rice while hot:** Drain the potatoes well, let them steam off for 3 minutes until just cool enough to handle with a towel, peel off the skins, and press immediately through a potato ricer (or food mill) into a wide bowl. Let the riced potatoes cool for 10 to 15 minutes until warm, not piping hot.
3. **Mix the dough gently:** Sprinkle the potato starch, salt and nutmeg over the potatoes, add the egg yolks and melted butter, and fold together quickly with your hands until a smooth, non-sticky dough forms. Do not overwork or knead like bread dough.
4. **Shape the dumplings:** Bring a wide pot of generously salted water (about 4 quarts / 4 liters) to a boil, then immediately lower the heat to a **very gentle simmer** (tiny bubbles only, never a rolling boil). With lightly dampened hands, divide the dough into 12 equal portions (about 3.5 oz / 95 g each). If using toasted croutons, press 2 to 3 cubes into the center of each portion and roll into a smooth, crack-free ball.
5. **Simmer until they float:** Slide the dumplings gently into the barely simmering water. Cover partially and let them steep at a gentle simmer for **18 to 20 minutes**—they will rise to the surface after about 10 minutes and swell slightly. Lift out with a slotted spoon, drain well, and serve immediately.

## Recipe 3: Crispy Herb-Garlic Roast Potatoes

If you prefer crunch over dumplings, parboiling starchy potatoes with a pinch of baking soda roughs up their exterior so they roast into shattering glass-crisp edges.

- **Prep time:** 15 minutes
- **Cook time:** 55 minutes
- **Total time:** 1 hour 10 minutes
- **Servings:** 6

### Ingredients

- 3 lb (1.35 kg) Yukon Gold or Russet potatoes, peeled and cut into 1.5-inch (4 cm) chunks
- 1 tbsp kosher salt (for the boiling water), plus 1 tsp flaky sea salt for finishing
- 1/2 tsp baking soda (breaks down potato edges for extra crunch)
- 1/3 cup (80 ml) rendered goose/duck fat or extra-virgin olive oil
- 3 cloves garlic, smashed
- 3 sprigs fresh rosemary

### Instructions

1. **Parboil with baking soda:** Place the potatoes in a large pot, cover with cold water by 1 inch (2.5 cm), add 1 tbsp kosher salt and the baking soda, and bring to a boil. Simmer **8 to 10 minutes** until a knife slips into the outside easily but the centers still have slight resistance.
2. **Preheat pan and rough up:** Preheat the oven to **425°F (220°C)** and put a large rimmed baking sheet with the oil or goose fat inside to heat for 10 minutes. Drain the potatoes well in a colander, let them steam dry for 3 minutes, then return them to the dry pot, cover with a lid, and shake vigorously for 15 seconds until the surfaces look rough and mashed.
3. **Roast crisp:** Carefully tip the potatoes onto the hot oiled sheet pan, toss to coat, and arrange in a single uncrowded layer. Roast **25 minutes**, flip each piece, add the smashed garlic and rosemary, and roast another **20 to 25 minutes** until deeply golden and crunchy. Finish with flaky sea salt.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Cook the braised red cabbage **1 to 3 days ahead**—its flavors meld dramatically overnight. You can also parboil and rough up the roast potatoes up to 8 hours in advance, store them chilled, and roast them right as your main comes out to rest. See our [make-ahead Christmas dinner guide](/en/blog/articles/make-ahead-christmas-dinner/) for more prep shortcuts.
- **Storage:** Cool leftover sides within 2 hours and refrigerate in airtight containers for **3 to 4 days** (USDA guidance). Braised red cabbage also freezes beautifully for up to 3 months. Slice leftover cold potato dumplings and pan-fry them in butter until golden crisp the next day—more ideas in [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — boiling potato dumplings:** A rolling boil tears potato dumplings apart in minutes. Keep the water strictly at a barely trembling simmer.
- **Common mistake — using waxy potatoes for dumplings:** Waxy red or fingerling potatoes stay gluey when mashed. Always choose floury, high-starch Russet/Idaho potatoes for dumplings.

## FAQ

### How far in advance can I make braised red cabbage?
You can make braised red cabbage up to 3 days before Christmas and keep it covered in the refrigerator, or freeze it up to 2 months ahead. Reheat gently on the stovetop with a small splash of apple cider or water.

### Why did my potato dumplings fall apart in the water?
Two causes are most common: the water was boiling too vigorously (it should barely simmer), or the potato dough was too wet because the potatoes were peeled before boiling. Boiling whole in their skins and adding 1 extra tbsp potato starch fixes both.

### Can I keep roast potatoes crispy if they finish early?
Spread them on a wire rack over a baking sheet in a turned-off oven with the door cracked slightly open for up to 15 minutes. Never cover crispy roast potatoes tightly with foil, which steams them soft.

### How many pounds of potatoes do I need per person for Christmas dinner?
Plan **1/2 lb (225 g) raw potatoes per adult** whether you are making dumplings, mashed potatoes or roast potatoes—round up slightly if your guests love seconds.
