The best Christmas dinner starters do two jobs at once: they make the first ten minutes at the table feel genuinely festive, and they leave everyone hungry for the main course. Heavy pastry puffs, cream-drenched dips, or oversized soup bowls sabotage the rest of the meal—and create an extra rush in the kitchen right when your roast needs attention.

The solution is choosing starters that are **bright, light, and made ahead**, requiring zero oven space while your main dish finishes. Below are five reliable Christmas starter ideas plus two complete recipes: a velvety **Roasted Butternut Squash & Apple Soup** (made 2 days ahead) and crisp **Smoked Salmon & Lemon-Dill Crostini** (assembled in 10 minutes).

## 5 easy Christmas dinner starters that fit any menu

1. **Roasted Butternut Squash, Apple & Sage Soup (recipe below):** Naturally gluten-free and easily vegan. Make it two days ahead, keep it chilled, and warm it on one back burner while the main rests.
2. **Smoked Salmon & Lemon-Dill Crostini (recipe below):** Bright, salty, crisp and cold—zero stove or oven needed on Christmas Day if you toast the baguette slices in the morning.
3. **Winter Citrus, Fennel & Pomegranate Salad:** Shaved fennel, blood orange or navel orange segments, bitter greens (radicchio or arugula), toasted pistachios and a Dijon-citrus vinaigrette. Ideal before rich [roast Christmas goose](/en/blog/articles/roast-christmas-goose-recipe/) or [roast duck](/en/blog/articles/christmas-roast-duck-and-turkey/).
4. **Whipped Goat Cheese with Roasted Grapes & Rosemary:** Creamy chèvre whipped with a splash of cream, topped with warm balsamic-roasted red grapes and served with crusty sourdough.
5. **Creamy Chestnut & Thyme Velouté:** Vacuum-packed roasted chestnuts simmered with shallots, garlic and vegetable stock, blended silky and topped with toasted bread croutons.

**Portion rule of thumb:** Keep soup starters to **3/4 to 1 cup (180 to 240 ml)** per adult and finger appetizers to **2 to 3 bites** per guest before a sit-down meal. For full course math, see our [Christmas dinner menu ideas](/en/blog/articles/christmas-dinner-menu-ideas/) and [holiday dinner planning checklist](/en/blog/articles/holiday-dinner-planning-checklist/).

## Recipe 1: Roasted Butternut Squash, Apple & Sage Soup

Roasting the squash and apples first concentrates their sweetness without adding heavy cream. A spoonful of apple cider vinegar at the end keeps the soup lively rather than cloying.

- **Prep time:** 20 minutes
- **Cook time:** 45 minutes
- **Total time:** 1 hour 5 minutes
- **Servings:** 8 starter portions (about 1 cup / 240 ml each)

### Ingredients

- 1 medium butternut squash, about 2.5 lb (1.1 kg), peeled, seeded and cut into 1-inch (2.5 cm) cubes
- 2 tart apples (Granny Smith or Honeycrisp), peeled, cored and cut into chunks
- 1 large yellow onion (about 7 oz / 200 g), cut into wedges
- 3 cloves garlic, unpeeled
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) extra-virgin olive oil
- 1.5 tsp kosher salt, divided, plus more to taste
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
- 4 cups (960 ml) low-sodium vegetable or chicken stock
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) heavy cream or full-fat coconut milk (optional, for extra richness)
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) apple cider vinegar
- **For garnish:** 1/3 cup (45 g) toasted pepitas (pumpkin seeds), 12 fresh sage leaves crisped in 1 tbsp olive oil or butter, and a swirl of cream

### Instructions

1. **Roast the squash and apples:** Preheat the oven to **400°F (200°C)** and line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment paper. Toss the cubed squash, apple chunks, onion wedges and unpeeled garlic cloves with 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp kosher salt and the black pepper. Spread in a single layer and roast for **30 to 35 minutes**, flipping halfway, until tender and caramelized at the edges.
2. **Simmer with stock:** Squeeze the soft roasted garlic out of its skins into a large soup pot. Add the roasted squash, apples, onions, nutmeg and 4 cups (960 ml) stock. Bring to a boil, reduce heat, and simmer gently for **10 minutes** so the flavors meld.
3. **Blend completely smooth:** Remove from the heat and purée with an immersion blender (or in batches in a countertop blender, venting the lid with a towel so steam can escape safely) until completely velvety.
4. **Finish and balance:** Stir in the cream or coconut milk (if using) and the apple cider vinegar. Taste and adjust with the remaining 1/2 tsp salt (or more) and black pepper. Thin with a splash of hot stock or water if thicker than you like.
5. **Crisp the sage and serve:** Heat 1 tbsp butter or olive oil in a small skillet over medium heat. Drop in the fresh sage leaves and fry for 20 to 30 seconds until dark green and crisp, then drain on a paper towel. Ladle warm soup into small heated bowls and garnish with crispy sage, toasted pepitas and a light swirl of cream.

## Recipe 2: Smoked Salmon & Lemon-Dill Crème Fraîche Crostini

Crisp toasted baguette rounds topped with tangy crème fraîche, silky cold-smoked salmon, bright lemon zest and capers—ready in minutes and universally loved alongside a glass of sparkling wine or [Christmas drinks](/en/blog/articles/mulled-wine-christmas-drinks-recipes/).

- **Prep time:** 15 minutes
- **Bake time:** 10 minutes (can be done hours ahead)
- **Total time:** 25 minutes
- **Servings:** 8 (24 crostini — 3 per guest)

### Ingredients

- 1 slender French baguette, sliced diagonally into 24 slices about 1/2-inch (1.2 cm) thick
- 3 tbsp (45 ml) extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 cup (240 g) crème fraîche (or full-fat whipped cream cheese softened with 2 tbsp plain yogurt)
- 1 tbsp grated fresh lemon zest, plus 1 tbsp (15 ml) fresh lemon juice
- 2 tbsp fresh dill, finely chopped, plus extra small fronds for garnish
- 2 tbsp fresh chives, finely snipped
- 1/4 tsp kosher salt and 1/4 tsp freshly cracked black pepper
- 8 oz (225 g) thinly sliced cold-smoked salmon, torn into bite-sized ribbons
- 2 tbsp drained nonpareil capers (optional)
- 1/4 small red onion, paper-thinly sliced (optional)

### Instructions

1. **Toast the crostini:** Preheat the oven to **375°F (190°C)**. Arrange the baguette slices in a single layer on a baking sheet, brush both sides lightly with olive oil, and bake for **10 to 12 minutes**, flipping once halfway, until golden and crisp around the edges (not rock-hard). Cool completely on the sheet.
2. **Whip the lemon-dill spread:** In a medium bowl, stir together the crème fraîche, lemon zest, lemon juice, chopped dill, chives, salt and black pepper until smooth. Taste and brighten with an extra squeeze of lemon if desired.
3. **Assemble right before guests arrive:** Spread about 2 tsp of the lemon-dill crème fraîche onto each cooled crostini. Fold a ribbon of cold-smoked salmon on top, add 2 or 3 capers, a sliver of red onion if using, and a tiny sprig of fresh dill. Arrange on a platter and serve immediately.

## Make-ahead, food safety and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Cook the Butternut Squash & Apple Soup up to **3 days ahead** (or freeze up to 2 months ahead—skip the dairy cream until reheating). For the crostini, bake the baguette rounds in the morning and store airtight at room temperature; mix the lemon-dill crème fraîche up to 24 hours ahead in the fridge, and assemble just 15 to 20 minutes before serving so the bread stays crisp. More multi-day strategies are in our [make-ahead Christmas dinner guide](/en/blog/articles/make-ahead-christmas-dinner/).
- **Food safety for cold-smoked salmon:** Keep smoked salmon continuously refrigerated at **40°F (4°C) or below** until assembly, and don't leave salmon crostini sitting at room temperature for more than **2 hours** (USDA / FDA guideline). Note that pregnant guests or immunocompromised individuals should skip cold-smoked fish unless heated until steaming—keep a few crostini topped with roasted red pepper or sautéed mushrooms as an easy alternative.
- **Storage:** Cool leftover soup within 2 hours and refrigerate for **3 to 4 days**. Leftover crostini toppings make a delicious next-day bagel breakfast—see [Christmas morning brunch ideas](/en/blog/articles/christmas-morning-brunch-ideas/) and [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — assembling crostini too early:** Topping toasted bread two hours ahead turns it chewy and soggy. Keep components ready and assemble right before guests arrive.

## FAQ

### How early should I serve Christmas appetizers before dinner?
Serve small bites or crostini 45 to 60 minutes before sitting down for dinner, and keep them light—around 2 to 3 pieces per person—so everyone still enjoys the main course.

### Can I make the Roasted Butternut Squash & Apple Soup vegan?
Yes. Use vegetable stock, crisp the sage leaves in olive oil instead of butter, and finish with full-fat coconut milk (or omit cream entirely—the roasted squash blends creamy on its own).

### What are the best cold Christmas starters if my oven is completely full?
Smoked salmon crostini, burrata with pomegranate and toasted pistachios, winter citrus-fennel salad, or chilled prawn cocktail need zero oven or stovetop space on Christmas Day.

### How much soup should I serve as a starter before Christmas dinner?
Serve about **3/4 to 1 cup (180 to 240 ml)** per guest in small warmed bowls or demitasse mugs. A full dinner-sized bowl leaves guests too full before the roast arrives.
