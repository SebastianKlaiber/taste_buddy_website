Store-bought Stollen is often dry, chalky, and loaded with waxy neon peel—which is why so many people are shocked the first time they taste a **homemade traditional German Stollen**. Real *Christstollen* is closer to a rich, buttery brioche packed with plump rum-soaked raisins, fragrant candied citrus, toasted slivered almonds, cardamom, and a tender ribbon of almond marzipan tucked inside, all sealed under a thick blanket of melted butter and powdered sugar.

Because Stollen actually needs **2 to 3 weeks to mature** so its butter, rum and spice flavors marry into the crumb, it is the very first thing you should bake in late November or early December—making your holiday week completely effortless.

## What keeps homemade Stollen moist (never dry)?

1. **Generous butter ratio:** Authentic German butter Stollen uses roughly **40% to 50% butter relative to flour weight**. That fat seals the crumb so slices stay rich and tender for weeks.
2. **Overnight fruit soak:** Soaking raisins and dried fruit in dark rum (or apple/orange juice) overnight prevents the fruit from stealing moisture out of the bread while baking and aging.
3. **The hot butter-and-sugar seal:** Brushing the loaf generously with melted butter the moment it leaves the oven and coating it thickly in powdered sugar locks in moisture like a natural glaze.
4. **Patience:** Fresh out of the oven, Stollen tastes like nice sweet bread. Wrapped tightly in a cool room for 14 to 21 days, it transforms into unmistakable Christmas Stollen.

## Complete Recipe: Traditional German Marzipan Stollen

- **Prep time:** 40 minutes (plus overnight fruit soak and ~2.5 hours rising)
- **Bake time:** 50 to 55 minutes
- **Maturing time:** 2 to 3 weeks (optional—you can also slice it after 24 hours if you can't wait, or see our Quark Stollen note below for same-day eating)
- **Yield:** 1 large loaf (about 20 to 24 slices)

### Ingredients

**For the overnight fruit soak:**
- 1.25 cups (200 g) golden raisins or sultanas
- 1/3 cup (60 g) finely chopped candied orange peel (or dried cranberries if you prefer less citrus peel)
- 1/4 cup (45 g) finely chopped candied lemon peel
- 1/3 cup (80 ml) dark rum (or freshly squeezed orange juice / apple juice for alcohol-free)

**For the rich yeast dough:**
- 4 cups (500 g) all-purpose flour (or German Type 405 / Type 550)
- 3/4 cup (180 ml) lukewarm whole milk (about 100°F / 38°C—warm to the touch, never hot)
- 1 packet plus 1 tsp (about 3.25 tsp / 10 g) active dry yeast (or 1 cube / 42 g fresh baker's yeast—heavy butter dough needs extra yeast power!)
- 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
- 1 cup (225 g) unsalted butter, softened at room temperature, cut into pieces
- 1/2 cup (60 g) blanched slivered almonds (lightly toasted and cooled)
- 1 tsp finely grated untreated lemon zest
- 1 tsp finely grated untreated orange zest
- 1 tsp pure vanilla extract (or 1/2 vanilla bean, scraped)
- 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
- 1/2 tsp ground mace or nutmeg
- 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1 tsp fine sea salt

**For the marzipan core:**
- 7 oz (200 g) almond marzipan paste (rolled into a 9-inch / 23 cm log)

**For the butter-and-snow coating:**
- 5 tbsp (70 g) unsalted butter, melted
- 1 cup (120 g) powdered sugar (confectioners' sugar), plus more for fresh dusting before serving

### Instructions

1. **Soak the fruit overnight:** The evening before baking, combine the raisins, candied orange peel and candied lemon peel in a bowl with the dark rum (or orange juice). Stir well, cover, and let soak overnight at room temperature so every piece plumps up. Drain any unabsorbed liquid the next morning and pat lightly dry with a paper towel.
2. **Start the sponge (Vorteig):** Place the 500 g flour in a large stand-mixer bowl and make a deep well in the center. Pour in the lukewarm milk, sprinkle the yeast and 1 tbsp of the sugar into the milk, and stir gently with a fork to pull in just enough flour to make a thin slurry. Cover with a clean towel and let rest in a warm spot for **15 to 20 minutes** until foamy and bubbly.
3. **Knead the butter dough:** Add the remaining sugar, softened butter, lemon and orange zests, vanilla, cardamom, mace/nutmeg, cinnamon and salt to the bowl. Knead with the dough hook on medium-low speed for **8 to 10 minutes** (or by hand for 12 minutes) until the dough is smooth, supple and pulls cleanly away from the bowl.
4. **Fold in fruit and nuts:** Turn the mixer to lowest speed (or work by hand on a clean counter) and gently fold in the drained rum-soaked fruit and toasted slivered almonds just until evenly distributed—avoid tearing the fruit.
5. **First rise:** Shape into a ball, place in a lightly greased bowl, cover, and let rise in a warm draft-free place for **1.5 to 2 hours** until nearly doubled in size. (Heavy butter dough rises more slowly than regular bread dough—be patient!)
6. **Shape with the marzipan log:** Punch the dough down gently on a lightly floured surface and pat or roll it into a thick oval roughly **10 × 12 inches (25 × 30 cm)**. Use a rolling pin to press a shallow lengthwise trough slightly off-center. Place the 9-inch (23 cm) marzipan log inside the trough. Fold the wider side of dough over the marzipan, tucking the edge gently so it overlaps cleanly into the classic asymmetrical Stollen hump. Tuck any exposed raisins inside the dough (exposed raisins burn bitter in the oven).
7. **Second rise:** Transfer the shaped loaf onto a parchment-lined baking sheet. Cover loosely with a kitchen towel and let rise for **45 to 60 minutes** until noticeably puffy. Meanwhile, preheat the oven to **350°F (175°C)**.
8. **Bake:** Bake on the lower-middle rack for **50 to 55 minutes**, until deeply golden brown and an instant-read thermometer inserted into the dough (not the marzipan center) reads **190°F to 195°F (88°C to 91°C)**. If the top browns too quickly after 30 minutes, tent loosely with aluminum foil for the remaining bake time.
9. **Seal with hot butter and powdered sugar:** The moment the Stollen comes out of the oven, poke 15 tiny holes across the top with a toothpick and brush generously all over (top and sides) with **half the melted butter**. Immediately sift half the powdered sugar over the warm butter. Wait 10 minutes, brush with the remaining melted butter, and coat thickly with the remaining powdered sugar until it looks snow-covered.
10. **Cool completely and mature:** Let the Stollen cool completely on a wire rack (at least 4 hours—never wrap warm!). Wrap tightly in parchment paper, then aluminum foil (or slide into an airtight tin), and store in a cool room (50°F–60°F / 10°C–15°C) for **2 to 3 weeks** before slicing. Dust with a fresh snowfall of powdered sugar right before serving.

## Storage, serving and common mistakes

- **Storage:** Properly butter-sealed and wrapped tightly in a cool, dry place (not the fridge, which dries starch faster), traditional Stollen keeps wonderfully for **4 to 6 weeks**. Once cut, press the two cut ends back together and wrap tightly—always slice from the center outward so the loaf stays moist! See our [Christmas cookie storage and baking timeline](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/) for when to bake Stollen relative to cookies.
- **How to serve:** Cut thin **1/2-inch (1.2 cm)** slices and serve at room temperature with strong coffee, black tea, or a warm mug of [mulled wine](/en/blog/articles/mulled-wine-christmas-drinks-recipes/). Leftover slightly stale Stollen makes extraordinary Christmas week French toast!
- **Common mistake — using hot milk:** Milk above 115°F (46°C) kills yeast instantly, leaving you with a dense brick. Milk should feel barely lukewarm on the inside of your wrist.
- **Common mistake — wrapping warm Stollen:** Sealing a warm loaf traps condensation, turning the snowy sugar shell soggy and inviting mold during maturation. Cool for a full 4+ hours first.
- **Common mistake — raisins poking out of the crust:** Exposed raisins char black in 55 minutes and taste bitter. Push any surface raisins gently down into the dough before the second rise.

## FAQ

### How far in advance should I bake German Stollen?
Bake traditional yeast Butter Stollen **2 to 3 weeks before Christmas** (late November to early December) so the butter, rum and spices infuse the entire crumb. It stays fresh for up to 6 weeks when tightly wrapped in a cool room.

### Can I make German Stollen without alcohol?
Yes. Soak the raisins and candied peel overnight in freshly squeezed orange juice, apple cider, or strong brewed black tea instead of dark rum—the fruit stays just as plump and flavorful.

### What if I don't like candied citrus peel?
Replace the candied orange and lemon peel 1:1 with dried cranberries, chopped dried apricots, or golden raisins, and add an extra teaspoon of fresh grated orange and lemon zest to keep that bright citrus perfume without chewy peel bits.

### Why didn't my Stollen dough rise much?
Rich butter-and-sugar doughs are heavy and rise slowly. Make sure your yeast is fresh, your milk is lukewarm (around 100°F / 38°C), and give the dough a full 1.5 to 2 hours in a warm spot (such as an off oven with only the oven light turned on).
