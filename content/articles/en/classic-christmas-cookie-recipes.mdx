Every December, ambitious holiday cookie lists quickly turn into five sticky bowls, half-used bags of nuts, and a kitchen dusted from floor to ceiling in flour. The smartest way to build an impressive holiday cookie tin is to combine **one rock-solid master butter cookie dough** that splits into multiple classic flavors with two or three distinct showstoppers—like nutty [Vanillekipferl](/en/blog/articles/authentic-vanillekipferl-recipe/), chewy gluten-free [Zimtsterne](/en/blog/articles/german-zimtsterne-cinnamon-stars/), or spiced [German Lebkuchen](/en/blog/articles/german-lebkuchen-gingerbread-recipe/).

Below is the classic German-style **1-2-3 Shortbread Butter Cookie Dough** (1 part sugar, 2 parts butter, 3 parts flour, enriched with egg yolk for clean edges)—the gold standard for cut-out cookies that hold sharp star, tree and heart shapes without puffing or spreading—plus how to turn one batch into **5 classic Christmas cookies**, including jam-filled Linzer stars.

## How to build a balanced Christmas cookie platter

The best cookie tins contrast flavors and textures so every bite feels different:

- **One crisp, buttery cut-out:** Classic Butter Cookies or Raspberry Linzer Stars (recipe below)
- **One tender, nutty melt-in-your-mouth cookie:** [Authentic Vanillekipferl](/en/blog/articles/authentic-vanillekipferl-recipe/) or [Spritz Butter Cookies](/en/blog/articles/spritz-butter-cookies-recipe/)
- **One soft, spiced cookie:** [German Lebkuchen](/en/blog/articles/german-lebkuchen-gingerbread-recipe/) or [Zimtsterne](/en/blog/articles/german-zimtsterne-cinnamon-stars/)
- **One inclusive batch for guests with dietary needs:** [Gluten-Free & Vegan Christmas Cookies](/en/blog/articles/gluten-free-vegan-christmas-cookies/)

Not sure which cookie to bake in week one versus week four? Check our [Christmas cookie storage and baking timeline](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/) so nothing goes stale before December 24.

## Master Recipe: Classic Butter Cut-Out Cookies & Jam-Filled Linzer Stars

This dough uses powdered (confectioners') sugar rather than granulated sugar for a finer, more tender crumb, and cold cubed butter worked in quickly so the cookies never spread into blobs in the oven.

- **Prep time:** 25 minutes (plus 1 hour chilling)
- **Bake time:** 9 to 11 minutes per tray
- **Total time:** about 2 hours
- **Yield:** about 55 to 60 cut-out cookies (or about 28 filled Linzer sandwich cookies)

### Ingredients

**For the master butter cookie dough:**
- 2.5 cups (300 g) all-purpose flour, plus a little extra for dusting
- 3/4 cup plus 2 tbsp (100 g) powdered sugar (confectioners' sugar), sifted
- 1 cup minus 2 tbsp (200 g) cold unsalted butter, cut into 1/2-inch (1 cm) cubes
- 2 large egg yolks (save the egg whites for [Zimtsterne](/en/blog/articles/german-zimtsterne-cinnamon-stars/)!)
- 1.5 tsp pure vanilla extract (or seeds scraped from 1/2 vanilla bean)
- 1 tsp finely grated untreated lemon zest
- 1/4 tsp fine sea salt

**For finishing (pick your style):**
- **For Classic Glazed Cut-Outs:** 1.5 cups (180 g) sifted powdered sugar whisked with 2 to 3 tbsp fresh lemon juice (or milk) until thick and glossy, plus festive sprinkles or chopped pistachios
- **For Jam-Filled Linzer Stars (Spitzbuben):** 3/4 cup (240 g) smooth seedless raspberry or red currant jam, warmed slightly, plus 3 tbsp powdered sugar for dusting

### Instructions

1. **Mix the dough cold and fast:** Combine the flour, sifted powdered sugar, lemon zest and salt in a large bowl (or food processor). Add the cold cubed butter, egg yolks and vanilla extract. Work everything together quickly with clean fingertips, a pastry cutter, or short pulses in the food processor just until clumps form, then press gently with your hands into a smooth dough. Stop kneading as soon as no dry flour streaks remain—overworking develops gluten and makes cookies tough.
2. **Shape into disks and chill:** Divide the dough into two equal flat disks (about 3/4 inch / 2 cm thick), wrap tightly, and refrigerate for **at least 60 minutes** (or up to 3 days). Flat disks chill evenly and roll out far more easily than round balls.
3. **Roll between parchment paper:** Preheat the oven to **350°F (175°C)** and line two baking sheets with parchment paper. Place one cold dough disk between two sheets of parchment paper (this prevents adding extra drying flour) and roll evenly to **1/8 inch (3 to 4 mm)** thick. If the dough softens while rolling, slide it on the parchment into the fridge for 10 minutes.
4. **Cut crisp shapes:** Cut out stars, trees, bells or circles with cookie cutters, dipping the cutter in flour if it sticks. For **Linzer cookies**, cut an even number of 2-inch (5 cm) fluted circles or stars, then punch a small 3/4-inch (2 cm) star or heart window out of the center of **half** the shapes. Transfer shapes to the parchment-lined trays with an offset spatula, leaving 1 inch (2.5 cm) between cookies. Gather scraps, re-chill 10 minutes, and re-roll once.
5. **Bake until pale gold:** Bake one tray at a time on the center rack for **9 to 11 minutes**, until the cookies are set and the very bottom edges show just a hint of pale golden color—do not let the tops brown dark, or they lose their delicate buttery flavor.
6. **Cool completely:** Let the cookies sit on the hot baking sheet for 3 minutes to firm up, then transfer to a wire rack until completely cool.
7. **Glaze or sandwich with jam:**
   - *For glazed cut-outs:* Brush or pipe the lemon icing over cooled cookies, add sprinkles immediately, and let set dry in a single layer for 2 hours before stacking.
   - *For Linzer cookies:* Dust the cut-out "top" cookies generously with powdered sugar **before** assembling. Spread 1/2 tsp slightly warmed raspberry or red currant jam onto the flat underside of each solid bottom cookie, then press a sugar-dusted top gently onto the jam. Let sit uncovered for 2 hours so the jam sets cleanly.

## Turn 1 batch of dough into 5 classic Christmas cookies

Split the dough right before chilling (1/2 recipe makes about 25–30 cookies) and fold in different mix-ins:

1. **Classic Lemon-Vanilla Cut-Outs:** Keep plain and brush with tart lemon powdered-sugar glaze.
2. **Raspberry or Red Currant Linzer Cookies (Spitzbuben):** Sandwich pairs with bright red jam and dust with powdered sugar.
3. **Almond-Orange Rounds:** Swap 1/2 cup (60 g) of the flour for finely ground blanched almonds and swap lemon zest for fresh orange zest; dip half of each cooled cookie in melted dark chocolate.
4. **Spiced Cocoa Cut-Outs:** Swap 3 tbsp (20 g) of the flour for unsweetened Dutch-process cocoa powder and add 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon; drizzle with melted white chocolate.
5. **Thumbprint Cookies (Engelsaugen):** Skip rolling; roll chilled dough into 1-inch (2.5 cm) balls, indent the center deeply with the back of a 1/2-tsp measuring spoon, fill with jam before baking, and bake 12 to 14 minutes.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Wrap the unbaked dough tightly and refrigerate for up to **3 days**, or freeze tightly wrapped disks for up to **2 months** (thaw overnight in the fridge before rolling).
- **Storage:** Store plain or glazed butter cookies in airtight metal tins layered with parchment paper in a cool, dry room for **3 to 4 weeks**. Jam-filled Linzer cookies soften gently from the jam and taste best within **10 to 14 days** (store them in their own tin so their moisture doesn't soften crisp cookies). See our complete [Christmas cookie storage guide](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/).
- **Food safety reminder:** As the FDA notes, both raw flour and raw eggs can carry bacteria such as *E. coli* or *Salmonella*—save the taste testing for after the cookies come out of the oven, especially when baking with kids.
- **Common mistake — using room-temperature butter:** Soft butter creates a greasy dough that spreads in the oven and erases crisp star points. Always start with cold cubed butter and chill cut shapes for 10 minutes before baking if your kitchen is warm.
- **Common mistake — overbaking:** Shortbread butter cookies firm up significantly as they cool. Pull them when the tops are still pale ivory and only the bottom rim is lightly blonde.

## FAQ

### Why do my cut-out Christmas cookies spread and lose their shape?
The dough was too warm going into the oven, or it contained baking powder. This classic butter dough uses **zero baking powder** and cold butter; chilling the cut-out tray in the fridge for 10 minutes right before baking guarantees razor-sharp edges.

### Can I use granulated sugar instead of powdered sugar in butter cookies?
You can, but granulated sugar creates a slightly crunchier, more rustic texture. Powdered sugar dissolves faster in cold butter and yields the smooth, melt-in-your-mouth crumb classic European butter cookies are known for.

### How early before Christmas should I bake classic butter cookies?
Bake plain butter cut-outs and Linzer cookies about **2 weeks before Christmas** (around December 10). Plain butter cookies keep fresh for up to 4 weeks in a cool metal tin, while jam-filled Linzer cookies actually get pleasantly tender after 2 to 3 days in the tin.

### What should I do with leftover egg whites from butter cookies?
Don't throw them away! Two or three leftover egg whites are the exact base needed for a batch of chewy gluten-free [German Zimtsterne](/en/blog/articles/german-zimtsterne-cinnamon-stars/) or coconut macaroons.
