Most Christmas cooking stress happens in a single frantic two-hour window right before dinner—trying to carve a roast, whip gravy, boil potatoes and dress a salad while guests stand in the kitchen asking if they can help. A **make-ahead Christmas dinner** flips that script completely: you choose dishes that actually *improve* overnight, finish 80% of the work by December 24, and spend Christmas Day reheating gently and enjoying your guests.

Below is a practical guide to what makes ahead best (and what doesn't), a clear 3-day prep schedule, and a complete recipe for **Red Wine Braised Beef Short Ribs**—the ultimate make-ahead Christmas main because chilling it overnight lets you lift off the hardened fat in one clean piece and makes the meat and sauce dramatically deeper in flavor.

## What makes ahead well for Christmas dinner (and what doesn't)

| Make Ahead 1–3 Days Before (Gets Better!) | Prep Components Ahead, Finish Day-Of | Always Cook Fresh Day-Of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Braised mains (beef short ribs, pot roast, beef cheeks, ragout) | Potato gratin (assemble Dec 24, bake Dec 25) | Quick green vegetables (green beans, shaved Brussels sprouts) |
| Cream soups (butternut squash, chestnut, mushroom) | Mashed potatoes (keep warm or reheat gently with butter/cream) | Crisp-skinned roasts (can be dry-brined ahead, roasted fresh) |
| Braised red cabbage & cranberry sauce | Salad dressings & toasted nuts (dress greens right before eating) | Dressed leafy salads & assembled crostini |
| Chilled desserts (tiramisu, panna cotta, cheesecake, chocolate mousse) | Stuffed pasta shells or lasagna (assemble & chill, bake Dec 25) | Yorkshire puddings or popovers |

## Your calm 3-day make-ahead Christmas dinner schedule

Pair this with a make-ahead starter like [Roasted Butternut Squash Soup](/en/blog/articles/christmas-dinner-starters-appetizers/), [Braised Red Cabbage](/en/blog/articles/classic-christmas-side-dishes/), and [Gingerbread Tiramisu](/en/blog/articles/christmas-dessert-recipes/):

- **December 23 (2 days before — heavy cooking day):**
  - Braise the Red Wine Beef Short Ribs (recipe below), cool quickly in shallow containers or an ice-water bath, and refrigerate overnight.
  - Cook the braised red cabbage and soup starter; chill once cool.
  - Bake any pie crusts or crisp cookies ahead (see our [Christmas cookie storage guide](/en/blog/articles/christmas-cookie-storage-timeline/)).
- **December 24 (1 day before — assembly & chilling):**
  - Lift the solid fat cap off the chilled short ribs and strain the sauce so it's velvety and ready to reheat.
  - Assemble your make-ahead dessert (tiramisu, panna cotta or chocolate mousse) and refrigerate overnight.
  - Peel and submerge potatoes in cold water in a pot (up to 18 hours in the fridge), wash salad greens, whisk vinaigrette, and set the dining table.
- **December 25 (Christmas Day — ~45 minutes of easy reheating):**
  - **90 minutes before dinner:** Take the short ribs out of the fridge for 20 minutes, then slide the covered Dutch oven into a **325°F (165°C)** oven for 50 to 60 minutes until steaming hot all the way through (at least **165°F / 74°C** per USDA reheating guidelines).
  - **40 minutes before dinner:** Boil the potatoes for mashed potatoes or creamy polenta; gently reheat the soup and red cabbage on low stovetop burners.
  - **10 minutes before dinner:** Toss a crisp green salad with vinaigrette, set out warm plates, and serve.

## Complete Recipe: Make-Ahead Red Wine Braised Beef Short Ribs

Braised short ribs are practically impossible to dry out, require zero carving at the table, and taste unmistakably restaurant-level on day two.

- **Prep time:** 30 minutes
- **Cook time:** 3 hours 15 minutes (done 1 to 2 days ahead)
- **Day-of reheating:** 50 minutes unattended in the oven
- **Servings:** 6

### Ingredients

- 5 lb (2.25 kg) bone-in beef short ribs (English-cut, about 2 to 3 inches thick, trimmed of heavy surface fat)
- 1 tbsp (15 g) kosher salt (or 1.5 tsp fine sea salt), plus more to taste
- 1.5 tsp freshly cracked black pepper
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) neutral high-heat oil (avocado or canola)
- 2 large yellow onions (about 14 oz / 400 g), coarsely chopped
- 3 medium carrots (about 9 oz / 250 g), chopped into 1-inch chunks
- 3 stalks celery, chopped into 1-inch chunks
- 6 cloves garlic, lightly smashed and peeled
- 3 tbsp (45 g) tomato paste
- 2 tbsp (16 g) all-purpose flour (omit or swap with 1 tbsp cornstarch slurry at the end for gluten-free)
- 2.5 cups (600 ml) dry, full-bodied red wine (Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot or Côtes du Rhône)
- 2.5 cups (600 ml) low-sodium beef stock
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme, 2 sprigs fresh rosemary and 2 bay leaves (tied together with twine)
- 1 tbsp (15 ml) balsamic vinegar or Worcestershire sauce (for finishing)
- **For fresh gremolata garnish (optional but brightens the dish):** 2 tbsp finely chopped flat-leaf parsley, 1 tsp grated orange or lemon zest, 1/2 small garlic clove finely grated

### Instructions

1. **Season and preheat:** Pat the short ribs completely dry with paper towels and season generously all over with the kosher salt and black pepper. Preheat the oven to **325°F (165°C)**.
2. **Sear deeply in batches:** Heat the oil in a large heavy Dutch oven (6 to 7 quart / 6 to 7 liter) over medium-high heat until shimmering. Working in 2 or 3 uncrowded batches, sear the ribs on all meaty sides for **3 to 4 minutes per side** until deeply mahogany brown. Transfer seared ribs to a platter.
3. **Brown the vegetables and tomato paste:** Pour off all but 2 tbsp of fat from the pot. Reduce heat to medium, add the onions, carrots and celery with a pinch of salt, and cook **8 to 10 minutes** until softened and browned at the edges. Stir in the smashed garlic and tomato paste and cook **2 minutes** until the tomato paste turns brick-red.
4. **Dust with flour and deglaze:** Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir 1 minute. Pour in the red wine, scraping every browned bit off the bottom of the pot with a wooden spoon. Bring to a brisk simmer and cook **8 to 10 minutes** until the wine reduces by roughly one-third.
5. **Braise low and slow:** Pour in the beef stock and add the tied herb bundle. Nestle the browned short ribs (and any juices on the platter) back into the pot so they are mostly submerged—liquid should reach about three-quarters up the sides of the meat. Bring to a bare simmer on the stove, cover tightly with a lid, and transfer to the oven.
6. **Cook until fork-tender:** Braise for **2 hours 45 minutes to 3 hours 15 minutes**, until a fork slides effortlessly into the meat with almost no resistance.
7. **Chill overnight (the make-ahead secret):** Remove from the oven and uncover for 20 minutes to release initial steam. Transfer the ribs to a clean baking dish and strain the braising liquid through a fine-mesh sieve into a wide bowl (pressing the softened vegetables to extract flavor, then discarding solids). Cool both quickly (within 2 hours per USDA guidelines), then refrigerate covered overnight—or up to 3 days.
8. **Skim and reheat on Christmas Day:** The next day, lift off and discard the solid white layer of chilled fat from the surface of the sauce. Place the short ribs snugly in the Dutch oven (or a deep baking dish), pour the defatted sauce over them, cover tightly, and reheat at **325°F (165°C)** for **45 to 60 minutes** until bubbling hot and the centers reach at least **165°F (74°C)**.
9. **Finish and serve:** Transfer the ribs to a warm platter. If the sauce is thinner than you like, simmer it on the stove for 5 minutes until glossy; stir in the balsamic vinegar or Worcestershire sauce and check seasoning. Spoon the sauce over the ribs, scatter the fresh gremolata on top, and serve over creamy mashed potatoes or buttered polenta.

## Food safety, storage and common mistakes

- **Safe cooling before refrigerating:** Never put a giant scorching-hot Dutch oven straight into the fridge—it warms the fridge and keeps the center in the danger zone too long. Per USDA guidelines, cool large batches within **2 hours** by transferring ribs and strained sauce into shallower containers (or setting the pot in a sink with cold water and ice for 20 minutes) before refrigerating.
- **Reheating safely:** Always reheat make-ahead braised dishes to a rolling simmer / **165°F (74°C)** internal temperature before serving.
- **Leftovers:** Eat cooked short ribs within **3 to 4 days** of cooking (if cooked on Dec 23, enjoy leftovers through Dec 26–27) or freeze leftover shredded rib meat in its sauce for up to 3 months—fantastic tossed with pappardelle pasta later in winter. See more in [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/).
- **Common mistake — crowding the pan during searing:** Crowded meat steams grey instead of browning, costing you half the flavor of the sauce. Take an extra 6 minutes to brown in batches.
- **Common mistake — pulling the ribs too early:** Short ribs go from tough to tender right at the 2.5-to-3-hour mark. If a fork meets resistance, cover and give them another 25 minutes.

## FAQ

### How many days ahead can I make braised beef short ribs for Christmas?
You can braise short ribs **1 to 3 days before Christmas** and keep them refrigerated in their strained sauce, or freeze them completely cooked in the sauce up to 2 months ahead and thaw in the refrigerator for 48 hours before reheating.

### What vegetarian main can I make ahead for Christmas dinner?
Our [Wild Mushroom, Lentil & Chestnut Wellington](/en/blog/articles/vegetarian-vegan-christmas-mains/) can be filled and wrapped up to 12 hours ahead (with the filling cooked 2 days ahead), or a red-wine lentil and mushroom ragout can be braised 2 days ahead just like short ribs.

### Do make-ahead mashed potatoes reheat well?
Yes, if you add extra butter and warm milk/cream. Reheat covered in a slow cooker on "low" for 1 to 2 hours, or in a buttered baking dish covered with foil at 350°F (175°C) for 35 minutes, stirring halfway with a splash of warm cream.

### How can I make the short ribs sauce richer without heavy cream?
Straining and reducing the defatted braising liquid for 5 to 8 minutes concentrates the natural gelatin from the rib bones into a glossy, restaurant-style glaze with zero cream needed.
