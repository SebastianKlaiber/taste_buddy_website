Making homemade **mulled wine** (*Glühwein*) takes less than 20 minutes, fills your entire home with the aroma of a German Christmas market, and tastes worlds cleaner than bottled pre-mixed versions—which are often overly sweet and heavy on artificial spice extract.

There is really only one iron rule to great mulled wine: **never let it boil.** Above **172°F (78°C)**, alcohol begins to evaporate rapidly, delicate fruit aromas flatten out, and whole cloves turn harsh and bitter. Keep the pot gently steaming between **155°F and 165°F (68°C and 74°C)** and every mug tastes bright, velvety and balanced.

Below are two complete recipes—classic **German Red Glühwein** (plus a crisp White Glühwein swap) and a deeply aromatic **Alcohol-Free Spiced Apple-Berry Punch (*Kinderpunsch*)** that adults genuinely enjoy too.

## How to pick the right wine for mulled wine

Skip both the cheapest bottom-shelf jug and expensive cellar bottles. Heat softens delicate oak aging, so aim for a **clean, fruity, medium-bodied dry red wine** in the $10 to $15 range with low tannins:

- **Best red wines:** Dornfelder, Pinot Noir (Spätburgunder), Merlot, Grenache/Garnacha, or fruity Malbec. Avoid heavily oaked, high-tannin Cabernet Sauvignon, which turns astringent when warmed with cloves.
- **Best white wines (for White Glühwein):** Dry Riesling, Pinot Grigio (Grauburgunder), or Sylvaner—paired with pear slices, star anise, vanilla and lemon zest.
- **Whole spices only:** Ground cinnamon and ground cloves turn mulled wine muddy and gritty. Always steep **whole Ceylon cinnamon sticks, whole cloves, star anise and cardamom pods**, then strain cleanly.

## Recipe 1: Authentic German Red Mulled Wine (Glühwein)

Steeping the spices first in a small splash of wine (or citrus juice) with sugar builds a concentrated spice syrup before you add the rest of the bottle—meaning maximum holiday aroma with zero boiled wine!

- **Prep time:** 10 minutes
- **Steeping time:** 25 minutes
- **Total time:** 35 minutes
- **Servings:** 6 mugs (about 6 oz / 180 ml each)

### Ingredients

- 2 bottles (750 ml each; 6.25 cups / 1.5 L total) dry, fruity red wine (such as Merlot, Pinot Noir or Dornfelder)
- 1 large untreated organic orange, scrubbed well (zest peeled in wide strips with a vegetable peeler, avoiding bitter white pith, then juice squeezed—about 1/3 cup / 80 ml juice)
- 2 wide strips untreated organic lemon peel (no white pith)
- 1/3 to 1/2 cup (65 to 100 g) light brown sugar, raw cane sugar, or mild honey (start with 1/3 cup and adjust to taste)
- 2 whole **Ceylon cinnamon sticks** (low-coumarin cinnamon recommended by the German BfR)
- 6 whole cloves
- 2 whole star anise pods
- 4 green cardamom pods, lightly crushed with the flat of a knife
- 1/2 vanilla bean, split lengthwise (optional, adds a bakery-like roundness)
- **Optional spike ("mit Schuss"):** 1/4 cup (60 ml) dark rum, brandy or Amaretto stirred in off the heat right before serving

### Instructions

1. **Build the spice-citrus base:** Combine the freshly squeezed orange juice, wide orange and lemon peel strips, 1/3 cup (65 g) brown sugar, Ceylon cinnamon sticks, cloves, star anise, crushed cardamom pods, vanilla bean (if using) and just **1/2 cup (120 ml) of the red wine** in a large heavy saucepan or Dutch oven. Bring this small base to a gentle simmer over medium heat and let it bubble softly for **6 to 8 minutes** until the sugar dissolves and the spices smell fragrant and syrupy.
2. **Add the remaining wine gently:** Lower the heat to **medium-low** and pour in the rest of both bottles of red wine.
3. **Warm without boiling:** Heat slowly until the mulled wine is steaming hot—around **158°F to 165°F (70°C to 74°C)** if you check with a kitchen thermometer—and tiny wisps of steam rise from the surface. **Never allow it to simmer or boil.**
4. **Steep off the heat:** Turn off the heat, cover the pot with a lid, and let the mulled wine steep for **15 to 20 minutes** so the wine absorbs the warm spices cleanly.
5. **Strain, taste and serve:** Strain the mulled wine through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean pot or thermal carafe (removing the cloves and peel prevents bitterness if keeping warm). Taste and stir in extra sugar/honey or optional dark rum/Amaretto if desired. Rewarm gently if needed and ladle into heatproof mugs garnished with a fresh orange slice and cinnamon stick.

**White Mulled Wine Variation:** Swap the red wine for **2 bottles dry Riesling or Pinot Grigio**, use **1/2 cup (120 ml) apple cider** instead of orange juice, swap half the cloves for 3 slices of fresh ginger, and steep with lemon peel, star anise and Ceylon cinnamon.

## Recipe 2: Alcohol-Free Christmas Market Punch (Kinderpunsch)

Most non-alcoholic holiday punches are just overly sweet hot juice. Brewing a base of tart **hibiscus or berry herbal tea** alongside unfiltered apple cider and whole spices gives this drink real wine-like depth and tannin structure for kids, drivers and anyone skipping alcohol.

- **Prep time:** 10 minutes
- **Steeping time:** 20 minutes
- **Total time:** 30 minutes
- **Servings:** 6 to 8 mugs

### Ingredients

- 3 cups (720 ml) water
- 4 hibiscus, rosehip or berry herbal tea bags (gives deep ruby color and pleasant tartness)
- 3 cups (720 ml) unfiltered cloudy apple cider (or apple juice)
- 1 cup (240 ml) dark grape juice or tart cherry juice
- 1 untreated organic orange, scrubbed and sliced into thin rounds
- 2 whole Ceylon cinnamon sticks
- 5 whole cloves
- 2 star anise pods
- 1 to 2 tbsp (20 to 40 g) maple syrup or honey (optional—taste first, as apple and grape juice are naturally sweet)

### Instructions

1. **Steep the tea and spices:** Bring the 3 cups (720 ml) water to a boil in a large pot. Remove from the heat, add the hibiscus/berry tea bags, cinnamon sticks, cloves and star anise, cover, and steep for **10 minutes**.
2. **Remove tea bags and add juices:** Remove and discard the tea bags (keep the whole spices in). Pour in the apple cider, dark grape or cherry juice, and orange slices.
3. **Warm gently:** Heat over medium-low heat for **10 minutes** until steaming hot (do not boil aggressively, or the orange pith turns bitter). Strain through a fine sieve, taste and sweeten lightly with maple syrup or honey only if needed, and serve piping hot in mugs.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Both Glühwein and Kinderpunsch are actually *more* aromatic when made **1 day ahead**. Steep as directed, **strain out all whole spices and citrus peels completely**, cool within 2 hours, and refrigerate in glass bottles or a covered pitcher. Reheat gently to **160°F (71°C)** on the stove or in a slow cooker set to "warm" for a holiday open house!
- **Storage:** Strained leftover mulled wine keeps tightly sealed in the fridge for **3 to 4 days**; leftover Kinderpunsch keeps **3 to 4 days** refrigerated (USDA guideline). You can even reduce leftover strained red Glühwein by half on the stove to make an incredible warm spiced syrup for [Christmas desserts](/en/blog/articles/christmas-dessert-recipes/) or poached pears!
- **Common mistake — leaving cloves and orange slices in a slow cooker all night:** After 60 minutes of heat, citrus pith turns intensely bitter and cloves overpower everything. Always strain first, then float one fresh orange slice per mug as garnish.
- **Common mistake — using ground spices:** Ground cinnamon floats as dusty sludge on top of every mug. Stick to whole spices.

## FAQ

### What temperature should mulled wine be heated to?
Keep mulled wine between **155°F and 165°F (68°C and 74°C)**—steaming hot to drink, safely below the 172°F (78°C) point where alcohol evaporates rapidly and spices turn bitter.

### Can you keep mulled wine warm in a slow cooker for a party?
Yes! Make and strain the mulled wine on the stovetop first (discarding all cloves and peels), then transfer the strained wine to a slow cooker on the **"warm"** setting (not "high") for up to 2 to 3 hours.

### What is the difference between mulled wine and German Glühwein?
They are close cousins. Traditional German *Glühwein* focuses purely on dry red (or white) wine, citrus peel, sugar and whole warming spices (cinnamon, cloves, star anise, cardamom), whereas British mulled wine or Scandinavian *Glögg* often adds port, brandy, raisins and blanched almonds straight into the mug.

### What snacks pair best with mulled wine?
Sweet pairings include [German Zimtsterne](/en/blog/articles/german-zimtsterne-cinnamon-stars/), [authentic Vanillekipferl](/en/blog/articles/authentic-vanillekipferl-recipe/) and toasted spiced nuts; savory pairings include warm soft pretzels, aged Alpine cheese and [Christmas raclette](/en/blog/articles/christmas-raclette-and-fondue-ideas/).
