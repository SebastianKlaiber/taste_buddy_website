Christmas Eve is rarely the night you want to spend four hours managing a complicated roast. Between wrapping last-minute gifts, hosting arriving relatives, church services or family games, the smartest Christmas Eve tradition in the world is keeping December 24 simple—and saving the big cooking marathon for Christmas Day.

In Germany, roughly a third of households follow this exact wisdom every year by serving **Kartoffelsalat mit Würstchen** (German potato salad with warm sausages): the salad is made hours earlier so its flavors deepen, the sausages heat gently in ten minutes, and nobody ends up scrubbing roasting pans at midnight.

Below are five easy Christmas Eve dinner ideas you can pull off in under 30 minutes of active evening work, followed by a complete, deeply flavorful recipe for **Warm Southern German Potato Salad with Sausages**.

## 5 easy Christmas Eve dinner ideas

1. **German Potato Salad & Simmered Sausages (recipe below):** Naturally dairy-free, mayonnaise-free, made in the morning, and ready to serve in 10 minutes. Swap in plant-based bratwurst or smoked tofu sausages for vegetarian guests.
2. **One-Pot Creamy Tomato-Basil Soup & Grilled Gruyère Sandwiches:** Simmer the soup on December 23; on Christmas Eve simply reheat and toast crusty sourdough sandwiches in a skillet or sheet pan.
3. **Sheet-Pan Garlic-Herb Salmon with Baby Potatoes & Green Beans:** Everything roasts on one rimmed baking sheet in 25 minutes—festive enough for guests with almost zero cleanup.
4. **Slow-Cooker Beef Bourguignon or Mushroom Ragout over Egg Noodles:** Assemble in the morning, let it simmer gently all afternoon while the house fills with cozy aromas, and boil buttered noodles right before eating.
5. **Grazing Board + Warm Baked Brie:** Assemble a board with cured meats or roasted nuts, seasonal fruit, olives, pickled vegetables, warm crusty bread and a wheel of Brie baked with cranberry sauce and rosemary. Pair with [mulled wine or spiced cider](/en/blog/articles/mulled-wine-christmas-drinks-recipes/).

## Complete Recipe: Warm Southern German Potato Salad with Sausages

Unlike heavy mayo-based picnic salads, Swabian-style Southern German potato salad uses warm waxy potato slices dressed in hot savory broth, apple cider vinegar, tangy mustard and neutral oil. The warm potatoes drink up the dressing until the salad turns silky, glossy and intensely flavorful—what Germans call *schlotzig*.

- **Prep time:** 25 minutes (plus 1 to 2 hours resting for best flavor)
- **Cook time:** 30 minutes
- **Total time:** about 1 hour (active work on Christmas Eve evening: 10 minutes)
- **Servings:** 6

### Ingredients

**For the German potato salad:**
- 3 lb (1.35 kg) waxy potatoes (such as Yukon Gold, Red Bliss or fingerlings), similar in size
- 1 medium yellow onion (about 5 oz / 140 g), very finely diced
- 1.25 cups (300 ml) hot beef or vegetable broth
- 1/4 cup (60 ml) apple cider vinegar or white wine vinegar
- 2 tbsp (30 g) medium-sharp German mustard or Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1.5 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 tsp freshly cracked black pepper
- 5 tbsp (75 ml) neutral vegetable oil (sunflower or canola)
- 1/2 medium English cucumber, thinly sliced (optional, adds crisp freshness)
- 1/3 cup (15 g) fresh chives, finely snipped

**For the sausages:**
- 12 fully cooked frankfurters, Wiener sausages or knackwurst (2 per adult; or plant-based smoked sausages)
- Whole-grain mustard, sweet Bavarian mustard and fresh crusty bread rolls for serving

### Instructions

1. **Boil potatoes in their skins:** Scrub the potatoes and place them whole and unpeeled in a large pot covered with cold salted water. Bring to a boil and simmer **20 to 25 minutes** until tender when pierced with a paring knife (do not overcook so they stay sliceable).
2. **Peel and slice while warm:** Drain the potatoes well. As soon as they are cool enough to hold with a clean kitchen towel (still steaming warm), peel off the skins and slice the potatoes into thin **1/8-inch (3 to 4 mm)** rounds into a large mixing bowl. Warm potatoes absorb the dressing; cold ones let it slide off.
3. **Steep the onions in hot broth:** Bring the broth to a simmer in a small saucepan. Add the finely diced onion and simmer for 2 minutes—this takes away the harsh raw bite while keeping the onion aromatic.
4. **Mix the dressing:** Off the heat, whisk the apple cider vinegar, mustard, sugar, kosher salt and black pepper directly into the hot onion broth.
5. **Dress and fold gently:** Pour the hot broth mixture over the warm sliced potatoes. Fold gently from the bottom up with a rubber spatula (try not to mash the slices). Let sit for **15 minutes** so the potatoes drink up most of the liquid.
6. **Add oil and rest:** Drizzle in the neutral oil and fold gently again. Let the salad rest at room temperature for **1 to 2 hours** (or refrigerate if resting longer than 2 hours for food safety, then bring back to cool room temperature 30 minutes before serving). Stir in the sliced cucumber (if using) and snipped chives right before serving, and adjust salt, pepper and vinegar—potatoes absorb a surprising amount of seasoning as they rest.
7. **Warm the sausages without splitting them:** Bring a wide pot of water to a boil, then **turn the burner off completely**. Slide the fully cooked sausages into the hot, non-boiling water, cover with a lid, and let them steep for **8 to 10 minutes** until hot all the way through (USDA recommends reheating fully cooked sausages to steaming hot, **165°F / 74°C**, especially for high-risk guests). If you choose raw bratwurst instead, pan-sear and simmer until an instant-read thermometer reads **160°F (71°C)** for pork/beef or **165°F (74°C)** for poultry sausages.
8. **Serve:** Spoon the glossy potato salad onto plates alongside the warm sausages, mustard and crusty bread.

## Creamy mayo alternative (Northern German style)

If your family prefers a creamy potato salad, let the cooked waxy potatoes cool completely first, cut into 3/4-inch (2 cm) cubes, and toss with **3/4 cup (180 g) mayonnaise**, **1/3 cup (80 g) plain yogurt or sour cream**, **3 tbsp pickle brine**, **1 tbsp Dijon mustard**, **1/2 cup finely diced dill pickles**, **1 diced crisp apple**, **1 small finely minced onion**, salt, pepper and fresh dill. Keep refrigerated until serving.

## Make-ahead, storage and common mistakes

- **Make ahead:** Make the broth-based potato salad on the morning of December 24 (or the evening of December 23) so the flavors develop fully. If refrigerated overnight, stir in 2 to 3 tbsp warm broth before serving to restore its glossy texture.
- **Storage:** Per USDA food-safety guidance, don't leave potato salad (especially mayo-based versions) or sausages at room temperature for more than **2 hours**. Refrigerate leftovers promptly and eat within **3 to 4 days**—see [how to use up leftovers](/en/blog/articles/how-to-use-up-leftovers/) for smart next-day ideas.
- **Common mistake — boiling sausages:** Boiling frankfurters or Wiener sausages makes their casings burst open in seconds. Always steep them in hot water with the heat turned off.
- **Common mistake — using floury Russet potatoes:** Starchy potatoes disintegrate into mash when tossed with broth. Always buy waxy potatoes (Yukon Gold or red-skinned) for potato salad.

## FAQ

### Should German potato salad be served warm or cold?
Southern German broth-based potato salad tastes best slightly warm or at room temperature, which keeps the dressing silky and aromatic. Creamy mayonnaise-based potato salad should always be served chilled from the refrigerator.

### Can I make German potato salad the day before Christmas Eve?
Yes, both broth-based and mayo-based versions taste even better after chilling overnight. For the broth version, take it out of the fridge 30 to 45 minutes before dinner and refresh it with a couple of tablespoons of warm broth and fresh chives.

### What vegetarian sausages work well for Christmas Eve?
Smoked plant-based frankfurters, vegan knackwurst, or pan-seared smoked tofu sausages pair wonderfully with warm German potato salad and whole-grain mustard—just use vegetable broth in the salad dressing.
